I. Overview

Son of Huayna Capac by a Quito princess, Atahualpa commanded the veteran northern army. In 1532 his generals took Cuzco, captured Huáscar, and massacred his kin. Days later, camped at Cajamarcaʼs hot springs with 80,000 troops, Atahualpa entered the plaza with 6,000 unarmed retainers to meet Pizarro. Cannon fired, horses charged, 2,000–7,000 Incas died; the Sapa Inca was pulled from his litter alive — Pizarro needed him to rule through (WHE, Atahualpa).

From prison Atahualpa ordered Huáscarʼs murder, offered the famous ransom, and learned Spanish chess. But Pizarro, fearing rescue and needing gold distributed, tried him for treason and had him garrotted after baptism. His generals fought on; Manco Inca was crowned as puppet — then rebelled.

1532 — Brother Falls

Quisquis and Calcuchimac smash Huáscarʼs armies at Chimborazo and Quipaypan. Huáscar captured, Cuzco sacked by Quito troops. Atahualpa rests at Cajamarca — supreme — as Pizarro climbs from the coast with 168 men and 62 horses.

1532 — Ambush

Atahualpa carried on a litter, singing, with 6,000 retainers. Friar Valverde offers the Bible; Atahualpa throws it down. Cannon fires, horsemen ride from hiding. Plaza becomes slaughter. The Sapa Inca seized unhurt — the empireʼs head cut off in an hour.

1532–33 — Ransom and Murder

Atahualpa marks a line 2.75 m high in a 6x5 m room: fill it once with gold, twice with silver, and free. Porters carry Coricanchaʼs walls for months — 6 tons gold, 12 silver. Pizarro distributes, then garrottes him 26 July 1533 for “treason.” Largest ransom in history — paid, then murdered.
