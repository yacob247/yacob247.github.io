I. Overview

The Maurya Empire (c. 321–185 BC) united most of India for the first time. Chandragupta Maurya, guided by Chanakyaʼs Arthashastra, overthrew the Nandas, repelled Seleucus (treaty 305 — 500 elephants for the east), and ruled from wooden-walled Pataliputra. Grandson Ashoka conquered Kalinga (261 — 100,000 slain, says his own edict), converted to remorse and Buddhism, and carved 33 edicts on rocks and pillars from Kandahar to Mysore — tolerance, hospitals for beasts, “all men are my children” (WHE; Britannica).

II. State & Dharma

Arthashastra: 26 departments, spies as “institutions,” state mines, forests, brothels taxed. Megasthenes (Seleucid envoy) describes Pataliputraʼs 570 towers, 64 gates, moat. Ashokaʼs dharma — non-killing, respect to parents and sects, officers of righteousness — spread Buddhism to Sri Lanka (son Mahinda) and Central Asia; his lion capital is Indiaʼs emblem today.

III. Fall

Late Mauryas shrank to Magadha; Bactrian Greeks pressed; general Pushyamitra murdered Brihadratha during a parade (185 BC). Shungas kept Buddhism but persecuted monks (chronicles say). Ashokaʼs pillars stood — one moved to Delhi by a Sultan, deciphered 1837 by Prinsep.

305 — Treaty with Seleucus

Chandragupta cedes the east to Seleucus for 500 war elephants — which win Ipsus 301. Marries a Greek princess; Megasthenes comes as envoy and writes the Indica. Indiaʼs first world treaty.

Kalinga 261

261 BC
Click to reveal
261 — Remorse of Ashoka

Ashoka takes Kalinga: “150,000 deported, 100,000 slain.” Rock Edict 13: remorse seized him; he turns to dharma, bans hunts, builds hospitals. Conquestʼs most famous apology, carved in stone.

Lions Roar

c. 250 BC
Click to reveal
250 — Pillars Across Asia

Polished sandstone pillars, 15 m, lion capitals, edicts in Prakrit, Greek, Aramaic. “Let none kill.” Thirty survive; four-lion Sarnath capital is Indiaʼs flag emblem and coinage.
