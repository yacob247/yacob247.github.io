I. Overview

The Indus (Harappan) civilization (c. 3300–1300 BC) was the largest Bronze Age world — over a million sq km, cities of 40,000 on ruthless grids with covered sewers, wells in every third house, and the watertight Great Bath (12×7 m) at Mohenjo-daro. No palaces, no temples, no kings depicted — standardized bricks (1:2:4), weights and seals suggest merchant oligarchies, not pharaohs. The script (~400 signs on 4,000 seals) is unread; the “Priest-King” bust and “Dancing Girl” bronze are its faces (WHE; Britannica).

II. Cities & Trade

Dholaviraʼs reservoirs (16 m deep), Lothalʼs dock (worldʼs earliest?), Shortugai tin post in Afghanistan, lapis to Mesopotamia (Meluhha in Akkadian texts). Cotton — worldʼs earliest — buttons, dice, carts. No warfare depicted; Great Bath likely ritual.

III. Fall

c.1900 Ghaggar-Hakra dries, Mesopotamian trade collapses, cities de-urbanize east to Gujarat. No invasion — skeletons show no massacre. Arya migration follows centuries later. Script dies; Vedas carry no memory of the cities beneath their fields.

c. 2400 — Ships to Sumer

Trapezoid basin 214×36 m, sluice gates, warehouse. Bead factories (carnelian), ivory, shell. Meluhhan ships reach Ur; Akkadian texts list their dogs and gold.

c. 1900 — Rivers Move

Monsoon fails, Ghaggar dries, trade stops. Mohenjo-daroʼs streets fill with squatters, then silence. No war — wells silt, bricks robbed. Script forgotten within generations.
