I. Overview

Around 725 Lady Xook dedicated Temple 23 and carved three lintels inside its doors. Lintel 24: she kneels pulling a thorned rope through her tongue while Shield Jaguar holds a torch. Lintel 25: the blood smoke becomes a giant serpent rearing with a god in its jaws. Lintel 26: she hands her husband his jaguar war-helmet. No other Maya woman is shown so boldly giving her blood to feed gods and legitimise a king (British Museum).

725 — Her Temple

Built as her ritual house. Three door-lintels told her story: sacrifice, vision, investiture. She is named as “Lady Xook, lady of Yaxchilan” — rare prominence for a queen. The temple faces the river so canoes saw her gods first.

AD 726

726 — The Serpent Rises

Collecting blood on bark paper and burning it, she conjured Sak Yaxuun, the Vision Serpent. From its mouth emerges an ancestor-warrior with spear and shield. The message: her pain calls the dead kings to bless Shield Jaguarʼs wars.
