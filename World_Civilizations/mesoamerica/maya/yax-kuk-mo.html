I. Overview

On 5 Sept 426 a foreign lord “took the sceptre” at Copán. His bones — found 1990s under the Acropolis — show a stocky warrior with healed fractures, filed teeth, and Mexican ornaments. He married a local queen, built the Yehnal Motmot temple over his palace, and made a frontier valley into a holy kingdom. Every later Copán king, down to 18 Rabbit 300 years later, called himself “heir of Yax Kʼukʼ Moʼ” (WHE, Copan).

426 — The Stranger King

Arrived with central-Mexican “Teotihuacan” warriors in goggle-eyed helmets. Seized or married into the valley chiefdom. Raised Altar Q centuries later shows 16 kings handing him the staff — history written backwards to bless the line.

437 and 1990s — Found Intact

Buried in Hunal structure with jade pectoral, shell goggles, stingray spines. Bones show he walked with a limp from old spear wounds. Found intact by Penn archaeologists — proof the founder legend was flesh and blood.
