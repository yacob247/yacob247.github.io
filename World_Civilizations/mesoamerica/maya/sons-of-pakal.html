Kan Bʼalam II & Kʼan Joy Chitam

The two sons of Pakal the Great — one completed his fatherʼs temples, the other was dragged in bonds to Toniná.

I. Overview

Kan Bʼalam finished the Cross, Sun and Foliated Cross temples praising his father Pakalʼs gods — the finest theology in Maya glyphs. His brother Kʼan Joy Chitam inherited peace but lost a war with highland Toniná. In 711 Tonináʼs warriors seized the holy lord himself and carved him kneeling, wrists bound. Palenque paid ransom or mourned; the dynasty survived but never regained Pakalʼs glory (WHE, Palenque).

684–692 — Theology in Stone

Three temples on one plaza told how gods gave Palenque maize, kingship and war. Tablets of 100+ glyphs each — Palenqueʼs scribes at their peak. Kan Bʼalam danced as gods on their dedication days.

711 — King in Bonds

Toniná Monument 122 shows Kʼan Joy Chitam kneeling, arms tied, head bowed. His captor boasts “he chopped the gods.” Palenqueʼs stelae stop for a decade — the city in shock until a new king, Kʼinich Ahkal Moʼ Naab, restored order c.722.
