Itzamnaaj Bʼalam II “Shield Jaguar”

Warrior-king of Yaxchilán whose queensʼ bloodletting lintels are the most famous Maya carvings.

I. Overview

Shield Jaguar seized Yaxchilánʼs throne after an interregnum and held it 61 years. His sculptors carved his queens drawing blood — rope through tongue, thorns through lips — to conjure gods before battle. Lintel 26 shows his wife presenting him a jaguar helmet; Lintel 25 shows her pulling a thorned rope through her tongue while a two-headed serpent rears (WHE, Yaxchilan).

The Long Reign

681–742 — Sixty-One Years

Took power 681 aged ~30s, allied to Piedras Negras by marriage, warred up and down the Usumacinta. Captured Aj Nik of Lacanha, kings of Hix Witz and Motul. Died 742 aged 90+; 10-year interregnum followed before son Bird Jaguar took throne.

Lintels of Blood

AD 725–726
Click to reveal
725–726 — Art of Sacrifice

Temple 23ʼs three lintels show Lady Xookʼs three bloodlettings and Shield Jaguar preparing for war. Finest low-relief carving in Maya art — now in the British Museum. They taught the world that Maya queens bled as bravely as kings fought.
