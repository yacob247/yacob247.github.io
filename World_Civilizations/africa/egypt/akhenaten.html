I. Overview

Ancient Egypt (c. 3100–30 BC) is historyʼs longest civilization — 3,000 years under 170+ pharaohs on one river. Narmer united Upper and Lower Egypt c. 3100; Djoserʼs step pyramid (c. 2670, architect Imhotep) began stone architecture; Khufuʼs Great Pyramid (c. 2560, 146 m, 2.3M blocks) was earthʼs tallest building for 3,800 years. Middle Kingdom literature (Tale of Sinuhe), Hyksos invasion, then New Kingdom empire: Hatshepsut the woman-king, Akhenatenʼs sun revolution, Tutankhamunʼs gold tomb (found intact 1922), Ramesses IIʼs Abu Simbel and Kadesh, and Cleopatra VIIʼs fatal game with Rome (WHE; Britannica).

II. Nile, Gods & Afterlife

The Nileʼs July flood laid black silt — calendar of 365 days (12×30 + 5 godsʼ birthdays) timed it. Gods: Ra, Osiris (murdered king, judge of dead), Isis, Horus (pharaohʼs falcon), Thoth (writing), Anubis (embalming). Afterlife required a preserved body (70-day mummification), Book of the Dead spells, and a heart lighter than Maatʼs feather. Pyramids, Valley of Kings tombs, Karnakʼs 134 columns — all machines for eternity.

III. Kingdoms & Fall

Old Kingdom falls c. 2181 (drought, nomarchs); Middle restores to 1650 (Hyksos chariots enter); New Kingdom expels Hyksos, conquers to Euphrates (Thutmose III, 16 campaigns); Sea Peoples repelled by Ramesses III 1177; Libyans, Nubians (25th “black pharaohs”), Assyrians, Persians rule in turn; Alexander founds Alexandria 331; Ptolemies reign to Cleopatraʼs asp (or poison) 30 BC.
