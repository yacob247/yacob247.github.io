The Kingdom of Aksum

Fourth great power of the ancient world with Rome, Persia and China — Ezanaʼs stelae, first Christian state, keeper (say Ethiopians) of the Ark. Every term explained, every date a card.

I. Overview

Aksum (c. AD 100–960) ruled the Red Sea — ivory, gold, frankincense to Rome and India. Ezana (c. 320–360) raised 24-m stelae (tower-houses in stone), minted trimetallic coins, and converted to Christianity under Frumentius — among the first states so, before Rome. Prophet Muhammadʼs followers found refuge here (615, First Hijra); Ethiopians say Menelik brought the Ark to Aksum, still guarded at Maryam Tsion (WHE; Britannica).

II. Trade & Faith

Adulis shipped elephants for Ptolemies, gold for Byzantium; Kaleb invaded Yemen (525) for Christians. Monasteries (Debre Damo), Geʼez Bible, rock churches foreshadow Lalibela. Coins read “king of the Aksumites” in Greek and Geʼez — globalization in metal.

III. Fall

Persians take Yemen 575; Arabs take Red Sea 700s; trade dies; coins debase; capital drifts south to Agaw lands — Zagwe dynasty, Lalibela churches (12th c.). Aksumʼs obelisks still stand; one looted by Italy 1937, returned 2005.

c. 330 — Cross on Coins

Ezana baptized by Frumentius (shipwrecked Syrian, tutor turned bishop). Disc-and-crescent coins replaced by crosses. Stelae abandoned for churches — faith written in gold.

525 — Across the Sea

Kaleb sails to Yemen to save Christians from Jewish king Dhu Nuwas. Rules 50 years via viceroys; Abrahaʼs elephant army (Quranʼs “People of the Elephant”) marches on Mecca 570 — and fails.

615 — First Hijra

Muhammad sends followers to Christian Aksum: “a king who wrongs none.” Negus Ashama protects them, refuses bribes. Islamʼs first refuge — mosque of Negash, oldest in Africa.
