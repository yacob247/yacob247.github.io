I. Overview

The Phoenicians (c. 1500–300 BC) — Canaanites of the Levant coast — were the Mediterraneanʼs master traders and sailors. From Tyre, Sidon and Byblos they shipped cedar, purple dye (10,000 murex shells per gram), glass and wine from Cyprus to Spain and founded colonies — Kition, Utica, Gadir (Cádiz), and Carthage (814 BC, queen Elissa/Dido). Their 22-sign alphabet, spread to Greece c. 800 BC, is the ancestor of every Western script (WHE, Phoenicia; Britannica).

II. Ships, Trade & Alphabet

Biremes and triremes with mortise-tenon hulls; stars and currents navigated — Pharaoh Nechoʼs Phoenicians circumnavigated Africa c. 600 BC (Herodotus doubted their “sun on the right” — proof they did). Hanno sailed to Gabon with 60 ships. Byblos gave Greece byblos (book); papyrus, ivory, and the alphabet passed with it.

III. Carthage & Fall

Carthage outgrew Tyre: 300,000 people, 300 warships, empire in Sicily, Sardinia, Spain under Hamilcar Barca. Rome destroyed it in three Punic Wars (264–146 BC) — salt myth aside, the site was cursed. Tyre itself fell to Alexanderʼs causeway siege 332 BC; 8,000 killed, 30,000 enslaved.

Alphabet to Greece

c. 800 BC

c. 800 BC — Letters Sail

Greeks adapt Phoenician 22 consonants, add vowels — first true alphabet. Earliest Greek inscription (Dipylon oinochoe) copies Semitic shapes. Our ABC descends straight from Tyre.

Dido Founds Carthage

814 BC
Click to reveal
814 BC — Oxhide City

Elissa (Dido) flees Tyre, buys “what an oxhide covers” — cuts it to strips, claims a hill (Byrsa = hide). Carthage grows to rule the west. Virgil makes her Aeneasʼ lover; history makes her a founder.

Hannoʼs Voyage

c. 500 BC
Click to reveal
c. 500 BC — To Gabon

Hanno sails 60 ships past Gibraltar, founds colonies, meets “hairy people” (gorillas — first Greek word for them), turns back at Mount Cameroonʼs fires. Periplus inscribed at Carthage; Greek copy survives.

Tyre Besieged

332 BC
Click to reveal
332 BC — Alexanderʼs Causeway

Island Tyre defies Alexander 7 months; he builds a mole from the mainland (still there — Tyre is a peninsula today). 8,000 Tyrians killed, 2,000 crucified on the beach. Phoenicia becomes Greek.
