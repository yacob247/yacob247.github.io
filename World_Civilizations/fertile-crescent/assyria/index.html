Assyria

The greatest military power of the ancient Near East — iron weapons, siege engines, the library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh. Every term explained, every date a card.

At a Glance

Land

Period
Old Assyria c.2025; Middle 1365–1050; Neo-Assyrian Empire 911–609 BC.
Army
Iron swords, cavalry, battering rams, siege towers; engineers, terror policy.
Kings
Tiglath-Pileser III, Sargon II, Sennacherib, Ashurbanipal.
Fall
Nineveh sacked 612 BC by Medes + Babylonians; Ashur 614.

I. Overview

Assyria (c. 2025–609 BC) built the first true military empire: professional standing army, iron weapons when rivals held bronze, cavalry and siege engineers who took any wall. Tiglath-Pileser III (744–727) invented deportation and provinces; Sargon II (721–705) built Dur-Sharrukin and took Samaria (722, Ten Lost Tribes); Sennacherib (704–681) sacked Babylon (689) and besieged Jerusalem (701 — “angel of the Lord” slew 185,000, says the Bible); Ashurbanipal (668–627) sacked Thebes and built Ninevehʼs library of 30,000 tablets — Gilgamesh preserved there (WHE, Assyria; Britannica).

II. War Machine & Rule

Provinces ruled by governors, annual eponym dating, royal roads with mule posts, mass deportations (4.5 million moved) to break nations. Palaces at Nimrud and Nineveh wore alabaster reliefs of lion hunts and battles — now British Museum. Terror was policy: flayings carved to deter revolt. Yet scribes preserved Sumerian classics, astronomers recorded eclipses, and engineers dug qanats and aqueducts (Jerwan, 50 km).

III. Fall & Legacy

After Ashurbanipal civil war weakened Assyria; Medes sacked Ashur (614), then Babylonians + Medes took Nineveh (612) after 3-month siege. Last king fell at Harran 609. Babylon inherited the empire; Persia inherited Babylon. The library survived — fire baked the clay — giving us Gilgamesh, omens and the King Lists.

744 — Empire Rebuilt

Tiglath-Pileser III seizes throne, creates provinces, standing army, deportations. Takes Damascus 732, Babylon 729. Assyria becomes a machine — every later conquest uses his system.

Samaria Falls

722 BC
Click to reveal
722 — Ten Tribes Lost

Sargon II takes Israelʼs capital after 3-year siege, deports 27,290 (his count). Ten Tribes vanish into Assyria — historyʼs most famous lost people. Judah alone survives under Hezekiah.

Jerusalem Besieged

701 BC
Click to reveal
701 — Angel of the Lord

Sennacherib traps Hezekiah “like a bird in a cage” (his prism), takes 46 Judean cities. Bible says plague/angel kills 185,000 Assyrians overnight; Herodotus says mice ate bowstrings. Jerusalem survives; Assyria turns to Egypt.

Library of Nineveh

668–627 BC
Click to reveal
668–627 — Clay Books

Ashurbanipal — literate king — collects 30,000 tablets: Gilgamesh, omens, medicine, star lists. Scribes copy in Old Babylonian script. Burned 612, baked hard — found 1849 by Layard. We read Assyria in its own words.

Nineveh Falls

612 BC
Click to reveal
612 — Three Months to Ashes

Medes + Nabopolassar besiege 3 months; Tigris floods break walls (prophet Nahum: “gates of rivers opened”). King Sin-shar-ishkun dies in flames. “Nineveh is laid waste — who will bemoan her?” Last stand Harran 609.
