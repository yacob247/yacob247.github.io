Biographies · Two Kings

Sennacherib & Ashurbanipal

The destroyer of Babylon — and the scholar who saved Gilgamesh.

I. Sennacherib (704–681 BC)

Son of Sargon II. Moved capital to Nineveh, built walls 12 km, palace “without rival,” aqueduct to Jerwan. Sacked Babylon 689 (diverted Euphrates, salted ruins) for killing his son. Besieged Jerusalem 701 — 46 cities taken, Hezekiah caged, then withdrew (plague says Bible). Murdered 681 by sons in Nineveh temple while praying — “cut down with the sword” (2 Kings 19:37) (WHE).

II. Ashurbanipal (668–627 BC)

Grandson; literate — “I read the stone inscriptions from before the Flood.” Sacked Thebes 663 (Egypt lost), Elam 639, brother Shamash-shum-ukinʼs Babylon 648. Built Nineveh library: 30,000 tablets — Gilgamesh, Enuma Elish, omens, medicine, Venus tables. Lion-hunt reliefs show him pouring libations over kills. Died 627; empire fell 15 years later. His books survived the fire that killed his empire.

668–627 — 30,000 Tablets
