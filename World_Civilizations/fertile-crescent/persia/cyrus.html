I. Overview

The Achaemenid Empire (c. 550–330 BC) stretched from the Indus to the Aegean — 5.5 million sq km, 40% of humanity. Cyrus the Great took Media (550), Lydia (546, Croesusʼ gold), Babylon (539, freed the Jewish exiles — Cyrus Cylinder, “first human rights charter”). Darius I organized 20 tribute satrapies, built Persepolisʼ Apadana stair, dug the Suez canal, and fought Marathon (490). Xerxes I bridged the Hellespont (480), sacked Athens, lost at Salamis and Plataea (WHE; Britannica).

II. Administration & Tolerance

Satrap + general + treasurer checked each other; Kingʼs Eyes inspected; Royal Road ran 2,700 km Susa–Sardis in 9 days by relay (“neither snow nor rain...” — Herodotus, later US Postal motto). Subject peoples kept gods and laws; Aramaic served all; darics paid armies. Persepolis reliefs show 23 nations bringing gifts — no slaves depicted, workers paid.

III. Wars & Fall

Greco-Persian Wars (499–449): Marathon 490, Thermopylae/Salamis 480, Plataea 479 — Greece free. Alexander, 20, crossed 334: Granicus, Issus (Darius flees), Gaugamela 331 (200,000 Persians beaten), Persepolis looted and burned 330 — revenge for Athens. Last Darius murdered by his men; Alexander marries Persian nobles, keeps satraps.

539 — Gate Opens

Cyrus diverts Euphrates, enters Babylon bloodlessly. Frees Jewish exiles, funds the Second Temple (Ezra). Cyrus Cylinder: “I restored the gods to their shrines.” Model conqueror — Xenophonʼs Cyropaedia schoolbook for 2,000 years.

490 BC

490 — Runnerʼs Legend

Darius sends 25,000 to punish Athens; 10,000 Athenians + Plataeans charge 8 stades, kill 6,400 for 192 lost. Pheidippides runs 42 km to announce — dies. Marathon’s mound still holds the 192.

480 — 300 and 371 Ships

Xerxes bridges Hellespont with flax cables, cuts Athos canal. Leonidasʼ 300 (+allies) hold 3 days; Artemisia advises; Themistocles lures 600 Persian ships into Salamis straits — 300 sunk. Invasion broken at sea.

331 — Empire Falls

Alexander 47,000 meets Darius 100,000+ on levelled plain. Companion wedge pierces centre; Darius flees (again). Babylon, Susa, Persepolis open gates. Persepolis burned — drunk revenge or policy, still debated.
