The Hittites

Anatoliaʼs empire of iron and chariots — rivals of Egypt at Kadesh, authors of historyʼs first peace treaty. Every term explained, every date a card.

At a Glance

Land
Central Anatolia (modern Turkey); capital Hattusa.
Period
Old Kingdom c.1650; Empire c.1400–1180 BC; Neo-Hittites to 700 BC.
Powers
Iron-working, 3-man chariots, law, diplomacy (Amarna letters).
Kings
Suppiluliuma I, Muwatalli II (Kadesh), Hattusili III (treaty).
Fall
Sea Peoples c.1180; Hattusa burned, abandoned.

I. Overview

The Hittites (c. 1600–1180 BC) ruled Anatolia from rock-cut Hattusa — lionsʼ gate, royal archives of 30,000 tablets in Indo-European Hittite (deciphered 1915). Suppiluliuma I (1344–1322) made Hatti a superpower, taking Syria and receiving a widowed Tutankhamunʼs queenʼs plea: “send me a son.” His son Muwatalli II met Ramesses II at Kadesh (1274) — 5,000 chariots, historyʼs largest chariot battle, claimed by both sides. Brother Hattusili III signed the Silver Treaty (1259) — first surviving peace treaty, in Akkadian and Hittite (WHE; Britannica).

II. Iron, Chariots & Law

Hittites worked iron when others held bronze (gifts of “good iron” to pharaohs); 3-man chariots (driver, shieldman, spearman) outfought Egyptʼs 2-man cars. Laws fined rather than killed (contrast Hammurabi); Telipinuʼs edict fixed succession; women held property. Rock reliefs at Yazılıkaya parade 90 gods in stone.

III. Fall

Sea Peoples + drought + revolts c. 1180 burned Hattusa; archives baked hard — found 1906 by Winckler. Neo-Hittite cities (Carchemish, Aleppo) lived on to Assyrian conquest 717. Their Indo-European tongue proved Anatolia once spoke Europeʼs family.

1274 — 5,000 Chariots

Muwatalli II ambushes Ramesses II with 3,500 chariots; pharaoh claims solo victory (Abu Simbel walls). Both sides claim triumph; Syria stays Hittite. Largest chariot battle ever.

1259 — First Peace

Hattusili III and Ramesses II sign eternal peace — extradition, alliance, gods as witnesses. Silver tablets exchanged; copy hangs in the UN today. Diplomacyʼs oldest charter.

1180 — Burned Capital
