I. Overview

Babylon (c. 1894–539 BC) rose twice. Hammurabi (1792–1750) united Mesopotamia and carved 282 laws on diorite. After Hittite sack (1595) and Kassite centuries, Nebuchadnezzar II (604–562) rebuilt Babylon as the largest city in the world — double walls 18 km, Ishtar Gate in blue glaze, Etemenanki ziggurat 91 m, and the fabled Hanging Gardens. He destroyed Jerusalem (586), deported Judah, and filled chronicles with tribute (WHE, Babylon; Britannica).

II. City & Empire

Old Babylon: Hammurabi's canals, Mari letters, Sippar sun temple. Neo-Babylon: Nabopolassar freed Babylon from Assyria (626), Nebuchadnezzar sacked Nineveh's heirs, ruled to Egypt's border. Population perhaps 200,000 — first city to exceed that mark. Cyrus entered 539 without battle; priests opened gates.

III. Religion & Learning

Marduk, city god, crowned king of gods in the Enuma Elish epic read each New Year. Scribes kept astronomical diaries — 800 years of nightly observations, basis of Greek astronomy. Mathematics, medicine (Diagnostic Handbook), and the 19-year calendar cycle were Babylonian gifts.
