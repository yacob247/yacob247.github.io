Sargon of Akkad

Cupbearer who conquered the world — 56 years, Gulf to Mediterranean.

At a Glance

Empire
Sumer, Elam, Syria to Mediterranean; capital Akkad (Agade).
Heirs
Sons Rimush, Manishtushu; grandson Naram-Sin (living god); daughter Enheduanna.

I. Overview

Sargon took Kish, then Uruk, Ur, Umma — “34 battles” — and made Akkadian the empireʼs tongue while Sumerian lived for priests. First standing army (5,400), first appointed governors, first dynasty claiming world rule. Empire fell to Gutians c.2154, but every later king — Hammurabi to Cyrus — called himself “king of Sumer and Akkad” after him (WHE, Sargon).

2334–2300 — World Taken

Lugalzagesi of Umma had united Sumer; Sargon destroys him, washes his sword in the Gulf, marches to the Cedar Forest and Silver Mountains. Empire: 800 km. Trade from Indus to Anatolia flows to Akkad.
