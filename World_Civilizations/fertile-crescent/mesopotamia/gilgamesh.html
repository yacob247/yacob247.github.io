I. Overview

Historical Gilgamesh probably ruled Uruk and built its 9-km wall. Literary Gilgamesh — tyrant tamed by wild Enkidu, mourner seeking life after Enkiduʼs death, listener to the Flood survivor Utnapishtim — asks what kingship is for. The Standard Babylonian version (c.1200, scribe Sin-leqi-unninni) ends where it began: “Look at Urukʼs wall” (WHE, Gilgamesh).

Epic — Wild Man Civilized

Gods make Enkidu to humble Gilgamesh; temple woman Shamhat civilizes him with bread, beer and love. The two wrestle, then embrace — the epicʼs heart: kings need equals, cities need wilds.

1872 — Flood Before Bible

George Smith reads Tablet XI: Utnapishtimʼs ark, dove, mountain-top — 1000 years before Genesis. Victorian England reels; Mesopotamiaʼs priority proved. Utnapishtim tells Gilgamesh: rest is for gods, not men.
