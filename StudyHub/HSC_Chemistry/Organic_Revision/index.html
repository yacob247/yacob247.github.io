Module 7 at a Glance

Organic Chemistry is the study of carbon-based compounds: how they are named, how their structures explain their properties, and how they react to form useful products. It divides into four inquiry questions:

  1. How do we systematically name organic compounds? — homologous series, functional groups, IUPAC nomenclature and structural and geometric isomers.
  2. How is organic chemistry used to make useful new substances? — properties such as melting and boiling points, solubility and reactions producing new compounds.
  3. What are the products of reactions of hydrocarbons and oxygen? — combustion, and the oxidation of alcohols to aldehydes, ketones and carboxylic acids.
  4. How can polymers be produced? — addition and condensation polymerisation, and the role of organic chemistry in producing useful materials.
Exam tip: Module 7 rewards precise language — always name the functional group, and when asked to "explain" a property, connect structure (chain length, branching, hydrogen bonding) to the observed behaviour, then to the trend. Marks are won on the link, not the list.
