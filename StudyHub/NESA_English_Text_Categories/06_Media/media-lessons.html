Stage 6 · Media · HSC English

10-Lesson Media Program

Ten lessons on reading media critically — each with a media-analysis task and an exam-style question.

Lesson 1

What Media Is and Does

Teaching focus: Define media texts; their purposes (inform, persuade, entertain) and how they shape perception.

Exam-style question: Identify the purpose and target audience of a given media text.

Starter sentences: “This text’s primary purpose is to…” · “It targets a… audience by…”

Activity: Bring in one media text each and sort them by purpose and audience.

Lesson 2

Reading Newspapers

Teaching focus: Layout, headlines, inverted pyramid and the difference between news and opinion.

Exam-style question: Analyse how a front page positions its reader.

Starter sentences: “The headline’s verb choices…” · “Placement on the page implies…”

Activity: Compare two front pages — note headlines, images and priorities.

Lesson 3

Deconstructing Magazines

Teaching focus: Cover codes, feature articles, pull-quotes and the co-constructed ideals of the magazine.

Exam-style question: Evaluate how a magazine cover constructs a lifestyle ideal.

Starter sentences: “The cover’s colour and caption promise…”

Activity: Analyse a cover’s headline, image and layout for the ideal being sold.

Lesson 4

Radio and Podcasts

Teaching focus: Voice, pacing, sound, interview technique, and how podcasts generate intimacy and authority.

Exam-style question: Analyse how sound and voice claim credibility in a podcast.

Starter sentences: “The host’s conversational tone…” · “Music and edits mark…”

Activity: Listen to a short episode and note how it builds trust and mood.

Lesson 5

Television News

Teaching focus: Bulletin structure, presenters, graphics, narration and visual pacing.

Exam-style question: Analyse how a news bulletin constructs the importance of a story.

Starter sentences: “The opening item signals…” · “Music and graphics frame it as…”

Activity: Watch one bulletin and rank stories by how they are presented as important.

Lesson 6

Representation and Stereotypes

Teaching focus: How media represents groups, and the effects of stereotyping and invisibility.

Exam-style question: Examine how a group is represented in a media text you have studied.

Starter sentences: “The repeated framing of… reinforces…”

Activity: Log the representation of a group across three media texts.

Lesson 7

Ideology and the ‘Norm’

Teaching focus: The taken-for-granted values that media presents as natural.

Exam-style question: Evaluate the ideology underpinning a media text.

Starter sentences: “Beneath its neutrality, the text assumes…”

Activity: Note the ‘unstated norms’ in an advertisement or article.

Lesson 8

Audience and Targeting

Teaching focus: Uses and gratifications; how advertisers and demographers build audiences.

Exam-style question: Discuss how a media text courts its audience and seeks to hold attention.

Starter sentences: “Constructed for a demographic that values…”

Activity: Profile the imagined audience of two contrasting media texts.

Lesson 9

Argument and Persuasion in Media

Teaching focus: Editorials, opinion pieces, debate formats and their rhetorical construction.

Exam-style question: Analyse the persuasive strategies of an opinion piece.

Starter sentences: “The editorial builds its case by…”

Activity: Write your own editorial, then trace its appeals to ethos/pathos/logos.

Lesson 10

Full Media Response & Reflection

Teaching focus: A sustained analytical response to media and a reflective evaluation of your own analysis.

Exam-style question: “All media constructs a version of reality.” Discuss with reference to at least two media texts.

Band 6 checklist: clear framing concept, precise media terminology, sustained judgement, evidence of original reading.

Activity: Write the response, then annotate it with media concepts you deployed well.

Marking guidance: Use NESA Stage 6 marking guidelines and align responses to the outcomes for viewing and analysing media.
