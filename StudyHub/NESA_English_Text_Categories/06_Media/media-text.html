The Complete Guide to Media

Reading the news with a critical eye — how media constructs reality, targets audiences and exercises power.

How to use this guide: Master the core concepts first, then apply them text by text. Use the 10-Lesson Program for practice.

Media Constructs Reality

Media texts select, shape and frame their subjects. The same event can be reported, and argued, in many ways. Key ideas:

  • Gateway: who decides what is newsworthy
  • Framing: the angle, emphasis and context given to a story
  • Selection & omission: what is included or left out
  • News values: what makes an event newsworthy — impact, proximity, conflict

Media Forms and Their Codes

FormCodes and conventions
NewspaperHeadlines, lead, inverted pyramid, captions, layout
MagazineCover appeal, feature article, pull-quotes, advertising
Podcast / radioVoice, sound design, pacing, interview, branding
TelevisionBulletin structure, narration, graphics, visual pacing

Audience and Positioning

  • How media targets a particular audience (ages, values, needs)
  • How it positions the audience to accept a point of view
  • The role of agenda setting — what we are told to care about
  • The hegemonies and norms assumed by mainstream media

Representation and Ideology

Media both reflects and shapes social attitudes. Ask of any representation:

  • Who is represented, how often, and in what roles?
  • What stereotypes are relied upon or challenged?
  • Whose voice centres and whose is marginalised?
  • What ideology does the media assume as ‘the norm’?

Ownership, Bias and Gatekeeping

  • Who owns the outlet and how does that shape content?
  • How do advertisers, sources and pressure groups influence?
  • How do editorials/opinion blend with reporting?
  • What responsibilities does journalism carry in a democracy?

How to Analyse a Media Text

  1. Identify the form, target audience and purpose
  2. Describe the codes and conventions used
  3. Analyse how the text constructs or frames its subject
  4. Examine representation, ownership and ideology
  5. Make a sustained judgement about its effect and value

Examples to Study

  • Compare how major Australian newspapers report the same event
  • Analyse a podcast’s construction of authority and intimacy
  • Deconstruct a magazine cover’s ideals and values
  • Track a news story across print, TV and social media
