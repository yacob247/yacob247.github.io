10-Lesson Film Program

Ten lessons from the shot to full-film analysis — each with close-viewing tasks and exam-style questions.

Lesson 1

Reading a Frame

Teaching focus: what a single shot communicates through framing, angle and composition.

Exam-style question: What does the shot and the camera angle convey about the character?

Starter sentences: “The low-angle shot constructs the character as…” · “The close-up isolates…”

Activity: Freeze a frame and annotate five elements that create meaning.

Lesson 2

Camera Angles and Power

Teaching focus: how height the camera encodes power, threat and empathy.

Exam-style question: Discuss the camera’s positioning of the audience in relation to a character.

Starter sentences: “Through alternating high and low angles…”

Activity: Find three shots that shift the viewer’s sympathy and explain why.

Lesson 3

Mise-en-Scène

Teaching focus: Setting, props, costume, colour and blocking build character and theme.

Exam-style question: Analyse how mise-en-scène in a scene communicates the film’s ideas.

Starter sentences: “The recurrent use of the colour … suggests…” · “The cluttered set conveys…”

Activity: Catalogue every element of mise-en-scène in one scene and assign it meaning.

Lesson 4

Editing and Montage

Teaching focus: How cuts, pacing and montage structure time, mood and argument.

Exam-style question: Analyse how editing shapes the pace and meaning of a sequence.

Starter sentences: “The rapid montage conveys…” · “The long take asks us to…”

Activity: Storyboard a sequence and label each cut.

Lesson 5

Sound and Music

Teaching focus: Diegetic/non-diegetic sound, the score, silence, and how audio directs emotion.

Exam-style question: Discuss how sound positions the audience in a scene.

Starter sentences: “The non-diegetic score signals…” · “The abrupt silence…”

Activity: Watch a scene twice — once silently — and name what the sound adds.

Lesson 6

Genre and Narrative

Teaching focus: How genre conventions and narrative structure organise expectation.

Exam-style question: Analyse how the film deploys or subverts a genre convention.

Starter sentences: “Playing with the conventions of the … genre…”

Activity: Map your film’s narrative onto the three-act structure.

Lesson 7

Character, Representation & Ideology

Teaching focus: Who is represented, how, and what ideology the representation assumes.

Exam-style question: Examine how the film constructs a social or cultural representation.

Starter sentences: “The film’s representation of… is shaped by…”

Activity: Note the voice, sympathy and agency of each character.

Lesson 8

Documentary: Truth as Construction

Teaching focus: how institutions, narration and selection claim ‘truth’.

Exam-style question: Evaluate how the documentary constructs a point of view.

Starter sentences: “Despite its realism, the film…” · “The narrator’s authority…”

Activity: Identify persuasive devices in a documentary excerpt and test their objectivity.

Lesson 9

Animation & Experimental Narrative

Teaching focus: The expressive freedoms and visual metaphors of animated and experimental film.

Exam-style question: Analyse how a stylistic choice in an animation conveys ideas differently from live action.

Starter sentences: “The visual metaphor of…” · “In animation, the impossible becomes…”

Activity: Compare the same idea depicted in live action and animation.

Lesson 10

Worded Film Analysis & Reflection

Teaching focus: Writing an integrated, scene-based film essay under exam conditions.

Exam-style question: Analyse how the film uses specific filmic techniques to convey its central idea.

Band 6 checklist: precise terminology, shows specific scenes, evidence & analysis in balance, thesis, fluent expression.

Activity: Write the response, then annotate it for how well you described shots.

Marking guidance: Use the NESA Stage 6 marking guidelines and align responses to course outcome for textual analysis.
