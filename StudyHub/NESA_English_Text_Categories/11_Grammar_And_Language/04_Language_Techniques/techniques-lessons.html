Lesson 1 · Foundation level

Imagery Basics: Simile & Metaphor

Intended learning: distinguish simile/metaphor and analyse the specific comparison each creates.

Hope is the thing with feathers — that perches in the soul.— Emily Dickinson, poem 254 (c. 186)

Teaching: Ask always: why THIS comparison? What does the vehicle (feathers/bird) lend the tenor (hope)? Fragility? Flight? Song?

Question (5 marks): Analyse Dickinson's central metaphor: what does the bird do for the idea of hope?

Band 6 sample

Dickinson's metaphor makes hope a bird small enough to perch yet stubborn enough to outlast "the chillest land" — the comparison argues that hope's power lies precisely in its modest persistence, not grand strength.

Feature Effect
"small enough to perch yet stubborn" Contradiction within vehicle analysed, not ignored.
"argues" Treats metaphor as thought, not decoration.
  • I named technique + quoted.
  • I unpacked both sides of the comparison.
  • I stated reader effect.

Worksheet 1

Find three metaphors/similes in your studied text; for each note vehicle, tenor, and one borrowed quality.

Lesson 2 · Core level

Extended Metaphor & Symbol

Intended learning: track sustained comparisons and symbols across a text.

Teaching: Extended metaphor organises whole passages; symbol condenses theme into object. Track Gatsby's green light across first mention, middle longing, final page.

Question (8 marks): Trace one symbol/motif through your text; show how meaning accumulates.

Band 6 sample

The green light begins as private longing, becomes "the orgastic future" itself by the final page — Fitzgerald scales the symbol until one dock-light carries the nation's self-deception about attainable tomorrows.

  • Evidence from 2+ points in text.
  • Growth of meaning traced explicitly.

Worksheet 2

Motif tracker: choose one recurring image; log every appearance with page ref + meaning-so-far column.

Lesson 3 · Core level

Personification & Pathetic Fallacy

Intended learning: analyse non-human agency and weather-as-mood conventions and subversions.

Teaching: Personification distributes power — what gains agency, what loses it. Pathetic fallacy externalises psyche ("the sky wept"). Watch for ironic use (sunny day at disaster = cruelty).

Question (6 marks): Analyse how personified elements reshape power relations in a passage.
  • Agency shift identified.
  • Mood link established.

Worksheet 3

Write a storm paragraph where weather mirrors a character's mind; underline your pathetic fallacy.

Lesson 4 · Core level

Sound Devices

Intended learning: hear alliteration/assonance/sibilance and connect sound to sense.

The fair breeze blew, the white foam flew, the furrow followed free.— Samuel Taylor Coleridge, The Rime of the Ancient Mariner (1798) — alliteration mimicking smooth sailing.
Question (6 marks): Explain how sound patterning enacts meaning in a supplied stanza.
  • Devices named precisely.
  • Sound-sense link argued, not asserted.

Worksheet 4

Colour-code one poem: blue=alliteration, pink=assonance, green=sibilance; annotate effects.

Lesson 5 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

Irony in All Three Forms

Intended learning: distinguish verbal/dramatic/situational irony and analyse audience positioning.

Teaching: Verbal = says opposite of means; dramatic = audience knows what character doesn't; situational = outcome contradicts expectation. Irony creates complicity or discomfort.

Stimulus: "The Gift of the Magi" (O. Henry, 1905) — double situational irony.
Question (7 marks): Identify the ironies in a supplied text and explain their combined thematic effect.
  • All forms correctly labelled.
  • Audience positioning explained.

Worksheet 5

Invent a one-paragraph story containing dramatic irony; mark where the reader knows more.

Lesson 6 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

Structural Techniques

Intended learning: analyse foreshadowing, flashbacks, circularity and frame structures.

Question (8 marks): Analyse how one structural choice shapes interpretation in a studied text.

Band 6 sample

Circular structure is fate made formal: when the ending's image repeats the opening word-for-word, the syntax of the whole text insists these characters were never going anywhere else — structure itself delivers the tragedy before any plot event confirms it.

  • Structure named + located.
  • Interpretive consequence drawn.

Worksheet 6

Draw your set text's shape as a diagram (linear? loop? frame?). Annotate two structural decisions and their effects.

Lesson 7 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

Persuasive Appeals

Intended learning: identify ethos/pathos/logos and analyse their orchestration.

Stimulus: any current op-ed; colour-code the appeals.
Question (8 marks): Evaluate how a composer balances evidence and emotion to persuade.
  • All three appeals located.
  • Judgement on balance offered.

Worksheet 7

Rewrite an emotional ad as pure logos; then as pure pathos; reflect which persuades you and why.

Lesson 8 · Advanced level

Visual Grammar

Intended learning: read salience, vectors, angles and colour using Kress & van Leeuwen's framework.

Question (6 marks): Analyse how composition directs viewer response in a supplied advertisement.
  • Semiotic terms used accurately.
  • Viewer positioning explicit.

Worksheet 8

Annotate a magazine ad: salience, vector(s), angle, colour palette, gaze demand/offering.

Lesson 9 · Trial HSC / Band 6 level

Technique Stacking

Intended learning: write paragraphs where multiple techniques serve one argument.

Teaching: Band 6 paragraphs are organised around IDEAS, not technique lists. Choose a claim; deploy 2–3 techniques as evidence for it.

Band 6 model paragraph

The poem renders grief as arrested motion. Its single long sentence never reaches a main verb, its dashes suspend closure mid-line, and its only full stop falls after a fragment — three techniques converging on one effect: language itself refuses to move on.

  • Paragraph led by idea, supported by techniques.
  • No 'technique spotting' list.

Worksheet 9

Write one stacked paragraph on your text: one claim, three techniques, zero summary.

Lesson 10 · Trial HSC / consolidation

Unseen Speed Analysis

Intended learning: extract techniques and effects from an unseen text in 10 minutes flat.

Teaching routine: 2-min read → margin-code devices (M=metaphor, S=sound, ST=structure) → pick the two richest → plan F-E-L sentences → write.

Assessment guidance: mirrors NESA Paper 1 unseen-text section criteria: analysis of how meaning is shaped. Verify current rubric at NESA.

Final self-check

Weekly timed drill until trials; keep a personal bank of your five best effect verbs and reuse them.
