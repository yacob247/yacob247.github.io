What Is Grammar?

Grammar is the system of rules a language uses to combine words into meaningful units. It has two sides: the formal side (the parts of speech and how they slot together) and the functional side (what each unit does in a sentence). In the NSW English K–10 Syllabus (2022), grammar is taught as a meaning-making resource, not a list of errors to avoid — students learn grammar so they can analyse how language positions readers, which is precisely what HSC markers reward.

Syllabus link: The NESA English K–10 Syllabus requires students to "understand and apply knowledge of language forms and features" and to use grammar to create texts for different purposes. Confirm outcomes for your stage at NESA K–10 English.
Grammar is the structural glaze of language: it fixes colour into pattern.— adapted from M.A.K. Halliday, An Introduction to Functional Grammar (1985)
