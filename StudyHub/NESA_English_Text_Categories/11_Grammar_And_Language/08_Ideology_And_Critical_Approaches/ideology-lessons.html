Lesson 1 · Advanced level

Ideology & Naturalisation

Intended learning: identify what a text treats as 'common sense' and interrogate it.

Teaching: Barthes' myth: ideology succeeds when invisible. Take a familiar advertisement or fairy tale; list every assumption it requires you to accept without noticing.

Stimulus: any current advertisement or a classic fairy-tale opening.

Question (8 marks): Identify two naturalised assumptions in a supplied text and analyse how its techniques make them feel inevitable.

Band 6 sample

The ad's grammar does the ideology: the product is Agent in every active clause ("it protects", "it delivers") while the family appears only as Goal — cared for, never caring. Protection becomes something purchased rather than performed, an assumption the viewer accepts because no sentence ever asks them to think it.

  • Assumptions named precisely.
  • Techniques linked to naturalisation.

Worksheet 1

'Common sense audit': list five assumptions of one text you love; star the one most worth challenging.

Lesson 2 · Extension / Trial HSC level

Marxist Lens I: Class & Money

Intended learning: trace how economic relations shape character fate and language.

Question (8 marks): Analyse how class determines voice and outcome in a studied text.

Band 6 sample

In Gatsby's world, accent is destiny: Tom's "I've been everywhere and seen everything" drawl performs inherited certainty, while Gatsby's studied "old sport" betrays a man rehearsing membership he can never hold. Fitzgerald puts class not in bank balances but in phonetics.

  • Economic reading grounded in textual detail.
  • No vague 'rich vs poor' generalising.

Worksheet 2

Wealth map of your text: who has money, how did they get it, what speech rights follow?

Lesson 3 · Extension level

Marxist Lens II: Hegemony

Intended learning: explain consent-manufacturing through Gramsci's hegemony using textual evidence.

Question (8 marks): How does a text show oppressed characters endorsing values that harm them?
  • Hegemony explained via specific scene/quote.
  • Text's own stance evaluated.

Worksheet 3

Find one moment where a character defends 'the system' against their own interest; annotate the language of that defence.

Lesson 4 · Extension / Trial HSC level

Feminist Lens

Intended learning: analyse gender construction via speech, agency and gaze.

One is not born, but rather becomes, woman.— Simone de Beauvoir, The Second Sex (1949)
Question (8 marks): Analyse how a studied text constructs or subverts gender roles through technique.

  • Agency distribution analysed (who acts/decides).
  • Gaze/silence patterns noted.

Worksheet 4

Speech-time audit of one scene: count lines by gender; note interruptions and topic control.

Lesson 5 · Extension / Trial HSC level

Postcolonial Lens

Intended learning: read representation, language privilege and resistance through Said/Fanon/Spivak.

Question (9 marks): Analyse how a studied text represents cultural encounter — who speaks, who is described.
  • Voice distribution examined.
  • Terminology accurate (othering, hybridity).

Worksheet 5

Two-column chart: characters with narrated interiority vs those only externally described. What pattern emerges across identity lines?

Lesson 6 · Extension level

Ecocritical Lens

Intended learning: assess whether nature is scenery, resource or kin in a text.

Question (8 marks): Analyse how human/non-human relations are figured in a studied text.

Band 6 sample

Transitivity again proves decisive: the river "gave" fish generously until the mill "took" — but after the dam, the river acquires verbs of refusal ("would not flood, would not feed"), grammatically promoted from resource to resistant actor at precisely the story's ecological turning point.

  • Nature's narrative status classified.
  • Linguistic evidence provided.

Worksheet 6

List every nature reference in one chapter; classify each: backdrop / resource / threat / agent.

Lesson 7 · Extension level

Psychoanalytic Lens

Intended learning: locate repression and its textual returns.

Question (8 marks): Identify a repressed desire/fear in a character and trace its symbolic returns.

  • Return-moments located (motif/dream/slips).
  • Mechanism explained, not just asserted.

Worksheet 7

Track one recurring image as symptom: each appearance = what unsaid thing resurfacing?

Lesson 8 · Advanced level

Reader-Response

Intended learning: use gaps and interpretive communities analytically.

Question (7 marks): Choose an ambiguous ending; explain two defensible readings and what each community values.

  • Both readings textually anchored.
  • Difference explained, not dismissed.

Worksheet 8

Gap inventory: list three things your text refuses to tell you; write the two readings each gap enables.

Lesson 9 · Extension / Trial HSC level

Braiding Lenses

Intended learning: combine two lenses without incoherence.

Teaching: Dominant lens carries thesis; second complicates one paragraph maximum. Transition formula: "Yet this economic reading cannot account for…"

Question (10 marks): Write one braided paragraph on your text (choose your pair).
  • Thesis remains single and clear.
  • Second lens genuinely complicates.

Worksheet 9

Lens-pair planner: dominant lens + complicating lens + the exact tension between them.

Lesson 10 · Trial HSC / consolidation

Lens-Driven Timed Essay

Intended learning: apply a chosen lens under exam conditions to an unseen-style question.

Assessment guidance: mirrors NESA Extension 1 criteria: sustained conceptual argument, textual integrity of analysis, sophisticated expression. Verify at NESA.
  • Lens integrated throughout, not name-dropped.
  • All evidence serves the ideological argument.
  • Sustained conceptual control.

Final self-check

Timed essay + self-mark against criteria; keep a personal 'theory phrase bank' of five sentences that earned marks.
