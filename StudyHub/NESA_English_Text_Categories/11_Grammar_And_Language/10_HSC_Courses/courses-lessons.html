Lesson 1 · All courses

Knowing Your Course

Intended learning: state your course's structure, modules and assessment weightings from memory.

Task: Complete a one-page course map for YOUR course from the guide; verify against NESA.

  • All modules listed with assessment types.
  • Official source cited.

Course map template + verification checklist.

Lesson 2 · All courses

Rubric Decoding

Intended learning: convert every rubric line into a marker behaviour you can practise.

Drill: Take your module rubric; write one 'marker action' per line; swap with a peer to test clarity.

Model conversion

"analyses skilfully how ideas are represented" → "every body paragraph names a technique, quotes it, explains its effect AND ties it to representation of an idea."

  • Conversions specific and actionable.

Worksheet 2

Rubric-to-action grid for your current module.

Lesson 3 · Standard/Adv/EALD

Common Module: Human Experiences

Intended learning: write about anomalies, paradoxes and inconsistencies precisely.

Teaching: Anomaly = deviation from norm; paradox = self-contradictory truth. These words must appear in analysis attached to evidence, not as decoration.

Question (10 marks): Analyse how your text represents one anomaly of human experience.

  • Term used accurately.
  • Individual + collective experience both addressed where relevant.

Worksheet 3

Anomaly hunt: three moments where characters act against expectation; note textual signals.

Lesson 4 · Standard focus

Standard Modules Strategy

Intended learning: build quote banks and paragraph skeletons for each Standard module.

Question (10 marks): Draft one full paragraph on identity/culture using two integrated micro-quotes.

  • Skeleton reusable across questions.

Worksheet 4

Fifteen-quote bank tagged by theme + technique.

Lesson 5 · Advanced focus

Advanced Modules Strategy

Intended learning: evaluate texts (not just analyse) and handle paired-text conversations.

Evaluation sentence bank

  • "The resonance lies less in theme than in form: both composers choose fragmentation, but only [later text] makes its reader assemble meaning — conversation completed by audience."
  • "Where [text A] naturalises its values, [text B] interrogates them — the dialogue exposes ideology as historical, not inevitable."
Question (10 marks): Write one comparative-evaluative paragraph on your paired texts.

  • Judgement explicit and justified.
  • Contextual change acknowledged.

Worksheet 5

Conversation matrix: themes × techniques grid comparing your two texts.

Lesson 6 · Standard/Advanced

The Craft of Writing

Intended learning: compose purposefully from stimulus and justify choices in reflection.

Stimulus: a quotation or image prompt.

Question: Compose a 250-word imaginative/discursive piece using the stimulus + 150-word reflection naming three deliberate choices.

Reflection model line

"I chose second person to implicate the reader in the protagonist's indecision; first person would have offered escape through distance that the piece needed to refuse."

  • Piece shows, not tells.
  • Reflection specific about alternatives considered.

Worksheet 6

Stimulus response log: five prompts, five micro-openings, one developed fully.

Lesson 7 · Extension 1

Literary Worlds Strategy

Intended learning: sustain a lens-driven argument and craft theory-embodied creative writing.

Two-part task: (a) critical paragraph using your dominant lens; (b) 300-word creative opening where the lens shapes form itself.

  • Lens integrated, not name-dropped.
  • Creative embodies concept structurally.

Worksheet 7

Lens selection sheet: pros/cons of each candidate lens for your elective texts.

Lesson 8 · Extension 2

Major Work Milestones

Intended learning: plan proposal, literature review, viva prep and reflection statement timeline.

Task: Draft a 200-word major work proposal: form, concept, intended audience, two key influences.

Viva prep tip

Prepare answers to: why THIS form? what does the literature say? what will success look like? Record decisions in the composer's journal immediately.

  • Proposal conceptually focused, not just topical.

Worksheet 8

Milestone calendar backwards-planned from due date.

Lesson 9 · EAL/D focus (+ transferable)

EAL/D Strategies

Intended learning: build effect-statement paraphrase fluency and listening-note systems.

Drills: (a) restate ten effects ten ways; (b) one timed listening practice with structured notes.

  • No repeated sentence frames across ten statements.
  • Notes capture stance + evidence.

Worksheet 9

Paraphrase ladder + listening template.

Lesson 10 · All courses

Trial & HSC Countdown

Intended learning: operate a six-week past-paper cycle ending in thesis-skeleton mastery.

Assessment guidance: align all self-marking to NESA published marking guidelines and feedback reports. Verify at NESA.
  • Weekly cycle: plan → write timed → mark vs criteria → drill weakest skill.
  • Final phase: skeleton memorisation only.
