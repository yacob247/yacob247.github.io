10-Lesson Analysis Program

Drill every stage of the pipeline until automatic.

Lesson 1 · All levels

Question Unpacking

Intended learning: convert any question into a task sentence in under 60 seconds.

Stimulus: bank of ten past-paper-style questions.

Drill: For each question: circle directive, box concepts, write one task sentence.

Worked example

Q: "How does the composer represent resilience to invite audience empathy?"
Task: "Show techniques constructing resilience AND explain the empathy mechanism."

  • No question misread across all ten.
  • Task sentences specific.

Worksheet 1

Directive verb glossary: define analyse/explain/evaluate/compare/discuss in your own words with one example each.

Lesson 2 · All levels

First Reading & Reaction Logs

Intended learning: capture reaction, turn and quotable line in three minutes.

Drill: Read a supplied passage once; complete the three-item log; compare logs across the class — divergences are interpretive gold.
  • All three items recorded fast.
  • 'Turn' justified.

Worksheet 2

Reaction log template printed five times for weekly use.

Lesson 3 · Core level

Annotation Code Fluency

Intended learning: code a passage in four minutes using the standard codes.

Drill: Same passage, shrinking time limits across the term: 6 → 5 → 4 minutes.
  • Codes accurate.
  • Only explainable items coded (triage rule).

Worksheet 3

Code legend card: keep in pencil case; add personal shorthand as it evolves.

Lesson 4 · Core level

Synthesis: Observations → Interpretation

Intended learning: derive a thesis from five annotations via the common-thread question.

Demonstration

Annotations: rain opens and closes scene; dialogue shrinks as tension rises; door imagery repeats. Thread: environment absorbs what characters cannot say. Thesis: "The text displaces unspoken grief into weather and architecture, arguing some losses exceed speech."

  • Thread genuinely common, not forced.
  • Thesis contestable.

Worksheet 4

Five-annotations-to-thesis worksheet on two different texts this week.

Lesson 5 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

Thesis Construction Speed Round

Intended learning: produce L3 theses in ninety seconds from cold texts.

Drill: Six rounds: new micro-text, 90 seconds, one thesis aloud/written.

  • Thesis answers implied question directly.
  • Escalates beyond topic statement.

Worksheet 5

Thesis ladder sheet: L1/L2/L3 versions for three texts.

Lesson 6 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

F-E-L Paragraphs at Speed

Intended learning: full paragraph in eight minutes flat.

Timed drill: 8 minutes: thesis given → write complete F-E-L paragraph on unseen text.
  • Feature named + evidence integrated.
  • Effect explained + link advanced.
  • No summary sentences.

Worksheet 6

Three timed paragraphs weekly until trials; track word count vs quality.

Lesson 7 · Trial HSC level

Unseen Prose Drills

Intended learning: execute the full unseen strategy on prose extracts.

Mock section: Two prose texts + questions, strict exam timing.

  • Timing respected.
  • Both answers address question keywords twice.

Worksheet 7

Error log from mock: categorise lost marks (timing? analysis? expression?).

Lesson 8 · Trial HSC level

Unseen Poetry & Visual Texts

Intended learning: adapt the pipeline for poems, stills and cartoons.

Mock section: One poem + one image pair, exam timing, comparison element included.

  • Poem: formal feature analysed.
  • Image: compositional feature analysed.

Worksheet 8

Visual checklist card: salience, angle, colour, gaze, text-image relation.

Lesson 9 · Trial HSC level

Comparative Questions Without Panic

Intended learning: structure comparisons by idea, not by text-blocks.

Teaching: Ban 'text A essay then text B essay'. Organise by shared concepts: similarity paragraph, divergence paragraph, evaluation paragraph.

Timed drill: Compare two short texts on one theme in 15 minutes.

  • Idea-led structure visible.
  • Both texts present in every section.

Worksheet 9

Venn → thesis worksheet for any pair of studied texts.

Lesson 10 · consolidation

Full Mock Section + Self-Marking

Intended learning: run an entire unseen-texts section then mark yourself honestly.

Assessment guidance: mirrors NESA Paper 1 marking guidelines: perception, textual reference, sustained composition. Verify at NESA.
  • /4 self-score logged weekly with trend noted.
  • Weakest check identified + drill assigned.

Final self-check

Maintain the /4 tracker through to trials; celebrate trend, not perfection.
