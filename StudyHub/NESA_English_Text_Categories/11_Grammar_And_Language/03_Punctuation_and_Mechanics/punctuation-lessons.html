Lesson 1 · Foundation level

Full Stops & the Power of Ending

Intended learning: end sentences decisively; analyse how full-stop placement shapes tone.

Teaching: A full stop is a decision about importance. What gets its own sentence gets its own weight.

Question (5 marks): Take a paragraph and re-punctuate it into shorter sentences; explain how emphasis shifts.

  • Correct sentence boundaries.
  • Explained weight shift.

Worksheet 1

Rewrite a run-on paragraph using only full stops and commas correctly.

Lesson 2 · Core level

Comma Rules 1–4

Intended learning: apply lists, coordinators, parentheticals and intro-element rules automatically.

Teaching: The four jobs (see guide). Test for parentheticals: remove the phrase — if the sentence survives, commas are correct.

Question (6 marks): Punctuate ten supplied sentences; justify each comma with rule numbers.
  • All four rules applied accurately.
  • Justifications cite the rule.

Worksheet 2

Write five sentences — one per rule plus an Oxford-comma ambiguity example.

Lesson 3 · Core level

Comma Splices & Repair

Intended learning: detect and fix splices instantly.

Teaching: Four fixes: full stop; semicolon; , FANBOYS; subordinate. Famous splices exist ("It was the best of times, it was the worst of times") — deliberate parallelism, not error; know the difference before imitating.

Question (7 marks): Fix eight splices two different ways each; state which fix suits formal essays best.
  • All repairs grammatical.
  • Register judgement justified.

Worksheet 3

Audit your most recent essay for splices; log each with its fix.

Lesson 4 · Core level

Semicolons & Colons

Intended learning: deploy semicolons for balanced tension and colons for announcement.

Teaching: Semicolon = full stop's softer cousin; colon = "here it comes". Both elevate essay register when used sparingly.

Model sentences

  • "The evidence was overwhelming; the verdict still shocked." (balanced tension)
  • "One fact undoes the argument: the study never replicated."
  • Complete clause before colon.
  • Related independents around semicolon.

Worksheet 4

Write three semicolon pairs and three colon announcements on any topic.

Lesson 5 · Advanced / Trial HSC level

Dashes & Dickinson

Intended learning: distinguish em/en dash functions; analyse dash as poetic technique.

I heard a Fly buzz — when I died —— Emily Dickinson, poem 591 (c. 186)

Teaching: Dickinson's dashes suspend closure mid-line and at line-end; readers hover between readings — the typography enacts uncertainty about death itself.

Question (8 marks): Analyse how dashes shape meaning in one Dickinson poem or equivalent text.
  • Treated punctuation as technique, not decoration.
  • Evidence quoted with dashes preserved.

Worksheet 5

Rewrite a Dickinson stanza without dashes; write 80 words on what is lost.

Lesson 6 · Core level

Apostrophes

Intended learning: master possession (incl. plurals) vs contraction; eliminate its/it's errors forever.

Question (5 marks): Correct twelve supplied apostrophe errors; explain the rule behind each cluster.
  • Zero unexplained corrections.
  • its/it's distinguished confidently.

Worksheet 6

Write ten possessive phrases across singular/plural/irregular forms.

Lesson 7 · Trial HSC / Band 6 level

Quotation Integration

Intended learning: embed quotes grammatically with minimal, purposeful quotation.

Teaching: Never dump. Integrate so the quote reads as part of your sentence; keep quotes under ~10 words where possible; square-bracket adjustments allowed.

Dump vs integrated

  • Dump: The author shows sadness. "He wept all night." This is sad.
  • Integrated: The night-long weeping — grief given duration rather than event — marks sorrow as the story's true setting.
  • Quotes flow within my sentences.
  • No orphaned quotes.

Worksheet 7

Integrate five quotes from your studied text; check grammar of each join.

Lesson 8 · Advanced level

Ellipsis, Brackets & Editorial Marks

Intended learning: use …, [ ] and [sic] honestly and skilfully.

Teaching: Ellipsis must not distort meaning; brackets host tense fixes ([lies] for [lay]) and [sic] flags source errors. Ethics of quoting: never change sense.

  • Omissions preserve meaning.
  • Adjustments bracketed correctly.

Worksheet 8

Shorten three long quotes to single powerful phrases using ellipsis + brackets lawfully.

Lesson 9 · Core level

Capitalisation, Italics & Titles

Intended learning: format text titles correctly (italics vs quotes) and capitalise properly.

Teaching: Novels/films/plays/newspapers = italics (Pride and Prejudice). Poems/short stories/articles/chapters = quotes ("The Raven"). Consistency beats house-style pedantry but NESA markers notice titles daily.

  • All titles formatted correctly in my essay.
  • Capitalisation consistent (Australian English).

Worksheet 9

Format fifteen mixed title examples correctly.

Lesson 10 · Trial HSC / consolidation

Punctuation as Technique: Timed Analysis

Intended learning: produce a timed paragraph analysing a composer's punctuation choices.

Band 6 model close

In this excerpt the exclamation mark arrives once — and its rarity is the point: amid flat declaratives, the single outburst isolates the moment the speaker loses composure, letting typography perform the breakdown the words themselves suppress.

Assessment guidance: aligns to NESA marking criteria for controlled writing and precise textual reference. Verify at NESA.

Final self-check

Timed task: 15 minutes, unseen passage, punctuation-focused paragraph; self-mark against criteria.
