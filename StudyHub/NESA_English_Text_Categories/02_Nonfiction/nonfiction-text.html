The Complete Guide to Nonfiction

From the four major forms to credibility and craft — how to read, analyse and write nonfiction at a Band 6 level.

How to use this guide: Work through the forms first, then the analytical toolkit. Test your understanding with the 10-Lesson Program.

The Persuasive Toolkit (Ethos, Pathos, Logos)

Most nonfiction, especially essays and speeches, uses the classical appeals of rhetoric to influence an audience.

  • Ethos (credibility): authority, trustworthiness, personal experience
  • Pathos (emotion): emotive language, anecdote, imagery that stirs feeling
  • Logos (logic): evidence, statistics, reasoning, counterargument

Analyse how a writer balances these and how the balance changes the effect. A speech may lean on pathos; a science essay on logos; a memoir on ethos gained through lived experience.

Credibility, Bias and Objectivity

  • Fact vs. opinion: distinguish verifiable claims from judgement
  • Source evaluation: who is quoted, why, and how selected
  • Biased tools: loaded language, selection, omission, conflation
  • Media ownership: how institutional interests shape the story

Analysing Structure

Nonfiction is deliberately shaped. Look for:

  • Opening hook: how the writer draws the reader in
  • Organising logic: chronological, thematic, problem-to-solution, compare-contrast
  • Transitions: how ideas and sub-points connect
  • Resolution / call: the final judgement or urging

Band 6 Approach to a Speech

Model analysis (Paul Keating’s Redfern Address, 1992): Keating opens by saying it was “a historic occasion” and that “we committed the murders.” The moral framing immediately establishes ethos, while the collective pronoun “we” implicates the audience directly. He builds pathos through the depiction of disadvantage in “the lowest life expectancy of any group in this country,” then shifts to logos by outlining a response. The speech’s strength is the seamless blending of accountability (“we brought the disasters”) with aspiration (“we can also fail at least the remedy”).

Biography: Fact, Selection and Story

Biography is never a neutral telling — it is a construction. Assess:

  • What the author chooses to include and omit
  • How chronology becomes a narrative (rise, fall, quest)
  • How sources shape a fair and how bias enters
  • The line between biography and hagiography
