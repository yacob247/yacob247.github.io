The Complete Guide to Digital & Multimodal Texts

Reading the grammar of the screen — modes, interactivity, navigation and the construction of the user.

How to use this guide: Learn the modes and the grammar of the screen, then adopt a method for analysing any digital text. Practise in the 10-Lesson Program.

The Five Modes

  • Written: words on screen and their typographic force
  • Visual: images, colour, layout and infographics
  • Audio: voice, music, sound effects
  • Gesture: movement, body and interface motion
  • Spatial: scrolling, framing and online architecture

The Grammar of the Screen

A digital text ‘says’ things through how it is arranged:

  • Layout: hierarchy of a page; what is given prominence
  • Colour and font: mood, brand and implied identity
  • Images and icons: instant, emotive meaning
  • Hyperlinks: pathways, choices and networks of meaning
  • Motion: animation and transitions that direct attention

Interactivity and the User

Unlike fixed texts, digital texts invite participation. The user chooses the path, and in doing so co-constructs meaning. Consider:

  • What choices does navigation offer, and what does it deny?
  • Where is the user positioned — visitor, member, consumer?
  • How do clicks, comments and likes make meaning?

A Method for Analysing a Digital Text

  1. Trace the modes: list the written, visual, audio, gesture, spatial modes used
  2. Read the layout: note hierarchy, colour, font and imagery
  3. Map navigation: what paths are offered, what is deep-linked
  4. Consider the user: who is positioned to act, and how
  5. Evaluate: how all the elements combine to create meaning and effect

Creating Your Own Multimodal Text

  • Begin with a clear purpose and audience
  • Choose modes that serve the message, not decorate it
  • Prototype the layout and navigation before polishing
  • Test the user experience by observing a partner using it
