10-Lesson Drama Program

Ten lessons from the elements of drama to staging a scene — each with teaching content, analysis tasks, and exam-style questions.

Lesson 1

Drama as Text + Performance

Teaching focus: The dual life of drama — on the page and on the stage; reading with performance in mind.

Exam-style question: Explain what a stage direction reveals that the dialogue alone cannot.

Starter sentences: “The stage direction instructs…” · “As a performed text…”

Activity: Annotate one page of a play with performing notes.

Lesson 2

The Elements of Drama

Teaching focus: Character, dialogue, structure, conflict, suspense and audience response.

Exam-style question: Identify the central conflict of the play and explain its development.

Starter sentences: “The central conflict between…” · “Dramatic tension builds when…”

Activity: Fill in a play template naming each element with an example.

Lesson 3

Structure of the Play

Teaching focus: Acts, scenes, the dramatic arc, and how the writer shapes time and information.

Exam-style question: Evaluate how the structure of the play builds dramatic tension.

Starter sentences: “The point of attack arrives when…” · “Retrospection in the play allows us to…”

Activity: Draw the plot arc of your studied play and label each turn.

Lesson 4

Dialogue and Subtext

Teaching focus: What characters say, what they mean, and the tension between the two.

Exam-style question: Analyse how subtext operates in a key exchange.

Starter sentences: “Beneath the surface of this exchange lies…” · “The interruptions suggest…”

Activity: Rewrite a scene’s subtext as an inner monologue for each character.

Lesson 5

Character and Motivation

Teaching focus: Goals, obstacles, wants versus needs, and how characters transform (or fall).

Exam-style question: Examine how one character’s desires drive the play’s action.

Starter sentences: “Driven by a need to…” · “The character’s tragic flaw…”

Activity: Write a character’s ‘wants’ and ‘needs’ list, then test against scenes.

Lesson 6

Genre and Style

Teaching focus: Tragedy, comedy, absurdism and realism — and how genre shapes expectation and meaning.

Exam-style question: Discuss how genre conventions shape the play’s effect.

Starter sentences: “As a tragedy, the play…” · “The absurdism is expressed through…”

Activity: Identify genre markers in an extract and predict the play’s conventions.

Lesson 7

Stagecraft and Design

Teaching focus: Set, lighting, sound, costume and blocking as meaning-makers.

Exam-style question: Analyse how a design choice conveys the play’s themes.

Starter sentences: “The bare set evokes…” · “Low-key lighting creates…”

Activity: Sketch a set design guru for a scene and justify each choice.

Lesson 8

Analysing a Performance

Teaching focus: Responding to a live or recorded production with precise, interpretive evaluation.

Exam-style question: Evaluate the director’s interpretation of a key scene.

Starter sentences: “In performance, the pacing…” · “The actor’s restraint suggests…”

Activity: Watch a scene and annotate three interpretive choices.

Lesson 9

Context and Drama

Teaching focus: Social, political and theatrical context of the play, and its relevance today.

Exam-style question: Show how the play engages with the concerns of its time.

Starter sentences: “Written against a backdrop of…” · “For a modernist audience…”

Activity: Research a play’s context and speculate on what a modern revival would emphasise.

Lesson 10

Full Response & Staging Reflection

Teaching focus: Sustained essay on drama and a creative/reflective staging task.

Exam-style question: “Drama is only complete in performance.” Discuss with close reference to your text and a production you have seen.

Band 6 checklist: confident command of dramatic terminology, integrated evidence, performance awareness, fluent expression.

Activity: Write the response, then reflect on which stagecraft choice most shaped meaning.

Marking guidance: Use NESA Stage 6 marking guidelines and align responses to the outcomes for reading and responding to drama.
