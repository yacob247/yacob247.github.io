The Complete Guide to Drama

Understanding drama as both script and performance — from the elements of drama to reading a scene and evaluating a production.

How to use this guide: Drama must be analysed as a living form. Read the script with a performance in mind, and treat stage directions as text. Combine suggestions with the 10-Lesson Program.

The Elements of Drama

  • Character: motivation, conflict, and development across the play
  • Dialogue & subtext: what is said vs. what is meant
  • Structure: acts, scenes, and the arc of the action
  • Conflict: the engine of drama — internal and external
  • Dramatic tension: how suspense and stakes are built and released
  • Audience: the play’s effect and its intended response

The Structure of a Play

Most plays follow a dramatic arc: exposition → point of attack → rising action → climax → falling action → denouement. Great drama often plays with these conventions through delay, denial and irony.

Note the significance of the opening (often establishing status quo, tone, and dramatic question) and the final scene (offering resolution, reversal, or open uncertainty).

Stagecraft

Drama’s meaning is realised through production choices. When analysing staging, consider:

  • Set and props: space, era, and symbolism of the design
  • Lighting and sound: mood, emphasis and time
  • Costume and props: character, class and transformation
  • Movement and blocking: spatial relationships and power
  • Pace and pauses: rhythm of the scene and its tension

How to Read a Scene

  1. Identify the dramatic question the scene must answer
  2. Track the shift in relationships and stakes from start to end
  3. Annotate stage directions for their instructions to actors
  4. Listen to subtext — what characters avoid saying aloud
  5. Consider the distribution of power and who changes

Evaluating Performance

When responding to a production, move beyond “I liked it.” Evaluate how the director, actors and designers make interpretive choices that realise (or challenge) the script, and what those choices add to the play’s themes in performance.
