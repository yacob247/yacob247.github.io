What Are Persuasive Texts?

Persuasive texts aim to convince an audience to accept a position, change a belief, or take action. Under the NSW English K–10 syllabus, NESA groups persuasive texts with arguments, debates, advertisements, editorials, discussions and opinion pieces. They rely on a clear contention, reasoned evidence, rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) and a deliberate call to action.

Unlike informative texts (which present facts) or imaginative texts (which create worlds), persuasion shapes facts and feelings toward a specific goal. At every stage K–10, students learn to recognise persuasive intent in the advertisements, opinion pieces and arguments they encounter, and to compose their own compelling cases.

Integrated with the NSW Syllabus

This pack aligns to the NSW English K–10 Syllabus (NESA), where students are taught to communicate, compose and respond to texts that argue and persuade. In understanding its primary purpose, teachers move from ‘forming an opinion’ in Early Stage 1 to ‘constructing structured arguments with evidence and rhetorical effect’ in Stage 5. Related curriculum guidance is available through the Australian Curriculum: English.
