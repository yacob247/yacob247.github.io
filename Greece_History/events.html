Every Major Event in Greek History

A dated catalogue of the decisive events — foundations, wars, battles, treaties, victories and defeats — from the Bronze Age to the fall of Constantinople, each with its significance.

Dating note. The events before c. 800 BC are dated approximately from archaeology and tradition; from the Archaic period onward, dates are those of the standard ancient and modern chronologies. BC dates count backward; AD dates forward.

1. The Bronze Age (c. 3200–1100 BC)

DateEventSignificance
c. 3200–2000 BCFlourishing of the Cycladic culture of the Aegean islands.Widespread use of marble figurines and seaborne trade; the earliest distinctive "Greek-adjacent" island culture.
c. 2000 BCBuilding of the first Minoan palaces on Crete (Knossos, Phaistos, Mallia, Zakros).Beginning of the first great palace civilisation of the Aegean and of European "palace" society.
c. 1900–1450 BCHeight of Minoan civilisation; use of Linear A script.Maritime trading empire; magnificent frescoes; the undeciphered Linear A records.
c. 1600 BCThe eruption of the volcano on Thera (Santorini).One of the largest eruptions in history; its ash and tsunami devastated the Aegean and buried Akrotiri; long debated how much it damaged Minoan Crete.
c. 1600–1450 BCRise of the Mycenaean civilisation on the Greek mainland.The earliest attested Greek-speakers built the citadels of Mycenae, Tiryns, Pylos; the shaft graves with their golden masks.
c. 1450 BCDestruction of the Minoan palaces; Mycenaean conquest of Crete.Crete passed to mainland Greek control; its script Linear A gave way to Mycenaean Linear B.
c. 1400 BCMycenaean expansion across the Aegean and to Cyprus and Anatolia.Establishment of a Mycenaean thalassocracy and long-distance exchange.
c. 1250–1180 BCThe (legendary) Trojan War; destruction of Troy VIIa by fire.The war of Homer's epics; the founding myth of Greek identity; Troy's archaeological destruction layer corresponds in date to the tradition.
c. 1200–1100 BCThe Bronze Age Collapse — destruction of the Mycenaean palaces; invasions; the "Sea Peoples."End of the palace societies and of Mycenaean Greece; collapse across the eastern Mediterranean; onset of the Greek Dark Ages.

2. The Dark Ages & Early Archaic Age (c. 1100–700 BC)

DateEventSignificance
c. 1100–900 BCThe Greek Dark Ages; loss of writing; population decline.Depopulation and the disappearance of Linear B; survival of oral epic poetry.
c. 900 BCGrowth of the Geometric style of Greek pottery, esp. at Athens.Recovery of craftsmanship and the earliest visual record of Greek life after the collapse.
c. 800 BCAdoption of the Phoenician alphabet by the Greeks.The creation of the first full alphabet (with vowels), enabling the return of writing and all later Greek literacy and literature.
776 BCTraditional founding date of the Olympic Games at Olympia.The games became the great panhellenic festival and the basis of Greek dating (the Olympiad).
c. 750–700 BCLife of Homer; composition/fixing of the Iliad and Odyssey.The foundational epics of Greek (and Western) civilisation; "the educator of Greece."
c. 700 BCLife of Hesiod; composition of the Theogony and Works and Days.The earliest systematic Greek theology and the first moral/agricultural poetry.
c. 750–550 BCThe Greek Age of Colonization (apoikiai on the coasts of the Mediterranean and Black Sea).Foundation of many Greek cities (Syracuse, Cumae, Byzantium, Massalia, Cyrene); spread of Greek culture and trade around the whole Mediterranean.

3. The Archaic Age (c. 700–499 BC)

DateEventSignificance
c. 657 BCCypselus becomes tyrant of Corinth.One of the earliest and most famous Greek tyrannies; founding of the Cypselid dynasty.
c. 621 BCDraco publishes the first written law code at Athens.Public, written law; the origin of the word "draconian."
594 BCSolon appointed sole archon of Athens; his reform programme.Abolition of debt-bondage and reform of the constitution; a landmark on the road to Athenian democracy.
c. 570–495 BCLife of Pythagoras at Croton.Founder of a philosophical-religious school; numbers as the principle of reality.
546 BCPeisistratus becomes tyrant of Athens.Stable popular tyranny; patron of art, religion and the cult of Dionysus (foreshadowing theatre).
c. 525–456 BCLife of Aeschylus.The "father of tragedy"; creator of the tragic form and the second actor.
514 BCMurder of Hipparchus, son of Peisistratus, by Harmodius and Aristogeiton.An event romanticised as the rise against tyranny; celebrated in Athens as the birth of democracy folklore.
510 BCExpulsion of the tyrant Hippias from Athens (with Spartan help).End of the Athenian tyranny.
508/507 BCReforms of Cleisthenes ("father of Athenian democracy").Reorganised the citizen body into ten tribes and gave sovereignty to the Assembly; the creation of Athenian democracy.
c. 500–428 BCLife of Anaxagoras.Ionian philosopher who introduced the concept of Mind (Nous) at Athens.
c. 500 BCLife and teaching of Heraclitus of Ephesus.Philosophy of flux and the logos.

4. The Persian Wars & the Fifth Century (499–431 BC)

DateEventSignificance
499 BCOutbreak of the Ionian Revolt under Aristagoras of Miletus.The eastern Greeks rose against Persia; the spark of the Persian Wars.
498/497 BCBurning of Sardis by the rebels (with Athenian and Eretrian aid).The event that won for Athens and Eretria the lasting enmity of Persia.
494 BCBattle of Lade; fall of Miletus to Persia.Crushing of the Ionian Revolt; Miletus destroyed.
492 BCFirst Persian invasion of Greece under Mardonius; storm off Mount Athos.Partial success and disaster by storm; the campaign aborted.
490 BCBattle of Marathon.Athenian and Plataean hoplites under Miltiades defeat the Persians under Datis; the first great Greek victory over Persia; origin of the modern marathon.
480 BCSecond Persian invasion under Xerxes; Battle of Thermopylae and Artemisium; sack of Athens; Battle of Salamis.The heroic last stand of Leonidas; the destruction of Athens; the naval victory of Themistocles that turned the war.
479 BCBattles of Plataea and Mycale.The defeat and destruction of the Persian army (Pausanias) and fleet; end of the Persian threat to Greece.
478 BCFounding of the Delian League.The alliance, dominated by Athens, that became the Athenian empire.
c. 466 BCBattle of the Eurymedon River (under Cimon).The League's great victory driving Persia from the Aegean.
462 BCReforms of Ephialtes; stripping of the Areopagus.Completion of radical Athenian democracy; Pericles' party takes control.
460–446 BCThe First Peloponnesian War (Athens vs. Sparta and allies).First clash of the two powers; ended by the Thirty Years' Peace (446 BC).
454 BCTransfer of the Delian League treasury to Athens.Formal transformation of the League into the Athenian empire.
447–432 BCBuilding of the Parthenon and the Acropolis monuments.The supreme monument of classical art, built with imperial tribute.
446 BCThirty Years' Peace between Athens and Sparta.A fragile truce whose breakdown would lead to the Peloponnesian War.
438 BCDedication of Phidias' gold-and-ivory Athena Parthenos.The crowning cult statue of the Parthenon; one of the wonders of classical art.
431 BCOutbreak of the Peloponnesian War (attack on Plataea by Thebes).The great war of Athens vs. Sparta that destroyed the balance of Greek power.

5. The Peloponnesian War & the Fourth Century (431–336 BC)

DateEventSignificance
430 BCOutbreak of the Great Plague in Athens.Killed perhaps a third of Athens' population, including Pericles (429 BC).
427 BCFall of Plataea to Sparta after siege.Destruction of an ally of Athens; emblematic atrocity of the war.
425 BCAthenian capture of the island of Sphacteria (Cleon, Demosthenes).A rare Athenian triumph; capture of Spartan prisoners.
422 BCBattle of Amphipolis; deaths of Brasidas and Cleon.The loss of both proponents of war, enabling the Peace of Nicias.
421 BCPeace of Nicias.A nominal fifty-years peace; in reality an uneasy truce.
415–413 BCThe Athenian Sicilian Expedition.The catastrophic defeat of the Athenian invasion of Syracuse; the turning point and ruin of Athens.
412 BCSpartan alliance with Persia (Persian money for a fleet).Persian gold tipped the naval balance against Athens.
411 BCOligarchic coup of the Four Hundred at Athens.Brief overthrow of the democracy; soon reversed.
410 BCAthenian naval victory at Cyzicus under Alcibiades.Recovery of Athenian fortunes at sea.
405 BCBattle of Aegospotami (Spartan victory under Lysander).Destruction of the Athenian fleet; end of the empire; the war decided.
404 BCSurrender of Athens; the Thirty Tyrants imposed.End of the Peloponnesian War; collapse of Athenian power; a brutal pro-Spartan oligarchy.
403 BCRestoration of the Athenian democracy.Overthrow of the Thirty; the democracy returned.
401–399 BCXenophon's March of the Ten Thousand into Persia.Revealed Persian weakness and the skill of Greek mercenaries.
399 BCTrial and death of Socrates at Athens.The martyrdom of philosophy; immortalised by Plato.
395–387 BCThe Corinthian War (Sparta vs. Athens, Thebes, Corinth, Argos).Fought with Persian support; ended by the King's Peace.
387 BCThe King's Peace (Peace of Antalcidas).Persia dictated terms; exposed Greek dependence on Persia.
c. 387 BCPlato founds the Academy at Athens.The first institution of higher learning in the Western world.
382 BCSpartan seizure of the Theban Cadmea.The oppressive act that provoked the liberation of Thebes.
379 BCLiberation of Thebes by Pelopidas and the exiles.Rise of Thebes as champion of Greek freedom; creation of the Sacred Band.
371 BCBattle of Leuctra.Epaminondas defeats the Spartans; the first defeat of a Spartan hoplite army; end of Spartan hegemony.
370–369 BCEpaminondas invades the Peloponnese; founding of Messene.Liberation of the Messenian helots; destruction of the economic basis of Spartan power.
359 BCPhilip II becomes king of Macedon.Beginning of Macedonian greatness.
357–356 BCPhilip II seizes the gold and silver mines of Mount Pangaeum.The wealth that funded the Macedonian army and empire; founding of Philippi.
355 BCOutbreak of the Third Sacred War (Phocis vs. the Amphictyonic League).Greece's self-inflicted crisis that let Philip intervene as champion of Delphi.
353 BCBattle of the Crocus Field (Thessaly).Philip's victory as defender of Apollo; his entry into Greek politics.
348 BCDestruction of Olynthus by Philip.End of the principal rival of the Macedonian foothold in the north.
346 BCEnd of the Third Sacred War; Peace of Philocrates.Philip gains a seat on the Amphictyonic Council; mastery over central Greece.
343 BCAristotle summoned to tutor the young Alexander.The education of the future conqueror.
338 BCBattle of Chaeronea.Philip defeats Athens and Thebes; the Sacred Band annihilated; the end of the independent great city-states.
337 BCCreation of the League of Corinth.United Greece under Macedonian hegemony; declared war of revenge against Persia.
336 BCAssassination of Philip II at Aegae.Alexander ascends the throne; the conquest of Asia begins.

6. Alexander the Great & the Hellenistic World (336–31 BC)

DateEventSignificance
335 BCDestruction of Thebes by Alexander.A terrifying example to Greece; only the house of Pindar spared.
334 BCAlexander crosses the Hellespont; Battle of Granicus.First great victory in Asia; beginning of the conquest of Persia.
333 BCBattle of Issus.Defeat of Darius III; capture of his family; Syria secured.
332 BCSiege and capture of Tyre (seven months).One of the greatest sieges in history; established Macedonian siege mastery.
332–331 BCAlexander in Egypt; founding of Alexandria; visit to the oracle of Ammon at Siwa.Foundation of the greatest Hellenistic city; Alexander hailed as son of Ammon.
1 Oct 331 BCBattle of Gaugamela.The decisive defeat of Darius III; end of the Achaemenid Persian Empire; Alexander "King of Asia."
330 BCBurning of Persepolis.The symbolic end of the Persian empire at its ceremonial capital.
327–326 BCConquest of Bactria and Sogdiana; marriage to Roxane.Alexander's eastern campaigns and his policy of reconciling the East.
May 326 BCBattle of the Hydaspes (vs. King Porus of India).Hard-won victory against war-elephants; Alexander's furthest conquest.
326 BCMutiny at the river Beas; retreat begins.The army refused to march further east; Alexander turned back.
325 BCMarch through the Gedrosian desert.The disastrous return phase, with huge losses.
10/11 June 323 BCDeath of Alexander the Great at Babylon.Death of the greatest conqueror of antiquity; the start of the Hellenistic Age.
323–281 BCWars of the Diadochi (successors).The partition of Alexander's empire into the Hellenistic kingdoms.
306/305 BCThe Diadochi take the royal title (Ptolemy I and others).Formal creation of the successor kingdoms.
301 BCBattle of Ipsus.Death of Antigonus I; final division of the eastern empire among the victors.
c. 300 BCFounding of the Museum of Alexandria; Euclid's Elements.The birth of the great research centre and of systematic geometry.
c. 247 BCCompletion of the Lighthouse of Alexandria (Pharos).One of the Seven Wonders; symbol of Hellenistic Egyptian glory.
280 BCFounding of the Achaean League (Peloponnese).The federal league that became a major Greek power against Macedon.
280–275 BCThe Pyrrhic War (Pyrrhus of Epirus vs. Rome)."Pyrrhic" victories; Roman resilience against a Greek king.
241 BCAttalus I takes the royal title at Pergamon.Rise of the Attalid kingdom.
223–187 BCReign of Antiochus III the Great (Seleucid).Brief reunification of much of the eastern empire.
197 BCBattle of Cynoscephalae.Roman victory over Philip V; end of Macedonian hegemony.
196 BCProclamation of Greek "freedom" at the Isthmian Games.Roman suzerainty over Greece announced by Flamininus.
191–190 BCRoman defeat of Antiochus III at Thermopylae and Magnesia.Break of Seleucid power in Asia Minor.
168 BCBattle of Pydna.Defeat and capture of Perseus of Macedon; the end of the Antigonid kingdom.
146 BCRoman sack of Corinth; dissolution of the Achaean League.The conquest of southern Greece and its organisation as the province of Achaea.
133 BCAttalus III of Pergamon bequeaths his kingdom to Rome.Establishment of the Roman province of Asia.
89–63 BCThe Mithridatic Wars (Mithridates VI vs. Rome).The last great Hellenistic resistance to Rome; finally crushed by Pompey.
63 BCRoman annexation of Syria by Pompey; end of the Seleucid Empire.The last of the great eastern Hellenistic kingdoms fell to Rome.
2 Sep 31 BCBattle of Actium.Octavian's victory over Mark Antony and Cleopatra; the last Hellenistic kingdom (Egypt) was about to fall; formal end of the Hellenistic Age.
30 BCSuicides of Cleopatra VII and Mark Antony; Roman conquest of Egypt.End of the Ptolemaic dynasty and of Hellenistic Egypt; completion of the Roman conquest of the Greek world.

7. Roman & Byzantine Greece (31 BC–AD 1453)

DateEventSignificance
44 BCJulius Caesar refounds Corinth as a Roman colony.Restoration of Corinth as the capital of Roman Greece.
c. AD 51–52Apostle Paul preaches in Corinth.Founding of the Corinthian church; his letters to it are part of the New Testament.
AD 324Constantine unites the empire and chooses Byzantium as the site of his new capital.Beginning of the new Christian capital.
AD 330Dedication of Constantinople ("New Rome").The founding of the greatest city of the medieval world and the seat of Byzantine civilisation.
AD 395Permanent division of the Roman Empire; the eastern (Greek) empire survives.The beginning of the Byzantine period proper.
AD 532–537Justinian I rebuilds the Hagia Sophia.The masterpiece of Byzantine architecture; symbol of Greek Orthodox Christendom.
AD 1204Sack of Constantinople by the Fourth Crusade.The ruinous blow to Byzantium by fellow Christians; never fully recovered.
29 May 1453Fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II.End of the Byzantine Empire and of the ancient Greek state; the beginning of Ottoman rule over Greece.
1830Greek independence from the Ottoman Empire (recognised by the Great Powers).The rebirth of the modern Greek state.
