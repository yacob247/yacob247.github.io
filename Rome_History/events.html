Every Major Event in Roman History

A dated catalogue of the decisive events — founding, wars, battles, treaties, reforms, assassinations and destructions — from the founding of Rome to the fall of the last Roman state, each with its significance.

Dating note. Events before c. 400 BC are known mainly from later tradition and are approximate; from the Punic Wars onward the chronology is securely established. BC dates count backward; AD dates forward.

1. The Monarchy (753–509 BC)

DateEventSignificance
753 BCTraditional founding of Rome by Romulus.The founding date of the city on the Palatine; the beginning of Roman history.
c. 750 BCThe "Rape of the Sabine Women" (tradition).The legendary union of Rome with the Sabines; founding of Roman institutions of marriage and the gentes.
715–673 BCReign of Numa Pompilius.Founding of Rome's religious institutions, calendar and priesthoods.
c. 630 BCFoundation of the colony of Rome on the coast (Ancus Marcius founds Ostia).Rome gains a port command at the mouth of the Tiber.
578–535 BCReign of Servius Tullius; the census and centuriate assembly.The reform that organised the army and citizen body by wealth; foundation of the later centuriate assembly.
509 BCExpulsion of Tarquin the Proud; founding of the Republic; first consulship of Brutus and Collatinus.Abolition of the monarchy; the birth of the Roman Republic and of republican "liberty."

2. The Early & Middle Republic (509–264 BC)

DateEventSignificance
c. 494 BCFirst Secession of the Plebs; creation of the tribunes.The plebeians win their champions; the beginning of the Struggle of the Orders.
c. 451–450 BCThe Law of the Twelve Tables.The first written code of Roman law; the foundation of the legal tradition.
c. 449 BCSecond Secession; fall of the Decemvirate (story of Verginia).Restoration of the consulship; consolidation of plebeian rights.
390 BCSack of Rome by the Gauls under Brennus.Destruction of the city; its recovery and rebuilding under Camillus ("second founder").
367 BCThe Licinian-Sextian laws.Plebeians admitted to the consulship; end of the patrician monopoly.
343–290 BCThe Samnite Wars.Roman conquest of central-southern Italy.
340–338 BCThe Latin War.Roman mastery of Latium; dissolution of the Latin League.
312 BCAppius Claudius builds the Appian Way.The first great Roman road; symbol of Roman engineering and of the road system.
287 BCThe lex Hortensia.Plebiscites (votes of the plebeian assembly) become binding on all; end of the Struggle of the Orders.
280–275 BCThe war against Pyrrhus of Epirus.Roman victory over a Greek king; mastery of Italy complete; "Pyrrhic victory" enters the language.

3. The Punic Wars & the Conquest of the Mediterranean (264–146 BC)

DateEventSignificance
264 BCOutbreak of the First Punic War.Rome's first war against Carthage; the conflict for Sicily that made Rome a Mediterranean power.
241 BCRoman naval victory at the Aegates Islands; end of the First Punic War; Sicily becomes a province.Rome's first province; Carthage's humiliating defeat that bred the Barcids' revenge.
218 BCOutbreak of the Second Punic War; Hannibal crosses the Alps.The invasion of Italy by the greatest general of antiquity.
217 BCBattle of Lake Trasimene.Hannibal destroys a Roman army in an ambush; death of Flaminius.
216 BCBattle of Cannae.The worst defeat in Roman history; Hannibal's double envelopment; Rome refuses to surrender.
212 BCRoman capture of Syracuse (Marcellus).The conquest of the great Greek city; death of Archimedes.
207 BCBattle of the Metaurus.Destruction of Hasdrubal's army; the doom of Hannibal's campaign in Italy.
202 BCBattle of Zama.Scipio Africanus defeats Hannibal; end of the Second Punic War; Carthage's power broken.
197 BCBattle of Cynoscephalae.Roman defeat of Philip V; end of Macedonian power in Greece.
196 BCProclamation of Greek "freedom" by Flamininus.Roman suzerainty over Greece began to be established.
190 BCBattle of Magnesia.Roman defeat of Antiochus III; end of Seleucid power in Asia Minor.
168 BCBattle of Pydna.Defeat and capture of Perseus; the abolition of the Antigonid kingdom of Macedon; Rome's mastery of the east complete.
149–146 BCThird Punic War.The final war against Carthage.
146 BCDestruction of Carthage; sack of Corinth.The end of Carthage and of Greek independence; Rome the unchallenged master of the Mediterranean.

4. The Late Republic & the Fall of the Republic (133–27 BC)

DateEventSignificance
133 BCTribunate and murder of Tiberius Gracchus.The beginning of a century of political violence in Rome.
123–121 BCReforms and death of Gaius Gracchus.Widening of the reform programme; the Senate uses force against a tribune.
107 BCMarius recruits the landless into the army; the Jugurthine War.Creation of the professional army; the rise of the powerful general.
102–101 BCMarius defeats the Cimbri and Teutones at Aquae Sextiae and Vercellae.Rome saved from the Germanic peoples; Marius at the height of power.
91–88 BCThe Social War.Revolt of the Italian allies; citizenship extended to them; rise of Sulla.
88 BCSulla marches on Rome.The first use of the army against the state; a fatal precedent.
82 BCSulla becomes dictator; the proscriptions.Mass killing of political enemies; Sulla's reform of the constitution.
73–71 BCThe slave revolt of Spartacus.The greatest slave rebellion; crushed by Crassus.
60 BCThe First Triumvirate (Caesar, Pompey, Crassus).The private alliance that broke the Senate's monopoly of power.
58–50 BCCaesar's conquest of Gaul.The addition of all of Gaul to the empire; Caesar's rise to supreme power.
52 BCBattle of Alesia; capitulation of Vercingetorix.The decisive victory in the Gallic War.
10 Jan 49 BCCaesar crosses the Rubicon.The beginning of the civil war against Pompey and the Republic; "the die is cast."
48 BCBattle of Pharsalus.Caesar's decisive defeat of Pompey; murder of Pompey in Egypt.
45 BCBattle of Munda.Caesar's final victory; sole rule over the Roman world.
15 Mar 44 BCAssassination of Julius Caesar (the Ides of March).Caesar is stabbed; the conspiracy intended to save the Republic instead doomed it.
43 BCSecond Triumvirate of Antony, Octavian and Lepidus; proscriptions (including Cicero).The civil war among Caesar's heirs; the silencing of the Senate.
42 BCBattle of Philippi.Defeat and death of the assassins Brutus and Cassius.
31 BCBattle of Actium.Octavian defeats Antony and Cleopatra; the end of the civil wars; the Republic at an end.
30 BCSuicides of Antony and Cleopatra; annexation of Egypt.Rome gains Egypt; Octavian is sole master.
27 BCOctavian granted the title "Augustus."Foundation of the Roman Empire and the Principate.

5. The Roman Empire & Pax Romana (27 BC–AD 180)

DateEventSignificance
AD 9Battle of the Teutoburg Forest (Varus); destruction of three legions by Arminius.Rome's failure to conquer Germany; the Rhine became the permanent frontier.
AD 14Death of Augustus; succession of Tiberius.The first peaceful succession of the empire.
AD 43Conquest of Britain by Claudius.Roman Britain added to the empire.
AD 60/61Revolt of Boudicca in Britain.A short-lived but violent rising crushed by Rome.
AD 64Great Fire of Rome (under Nero).Destruction of much of the city; Nero's rebuilding; the persecution of Christians.
AD 69The Year of the Four Emperors; rise of Vespasian.Civil war; foundation of the Flavian dynasty.
AD 70Destruction of the Temple of Jerusalem by Titus.The end of the Jewish revolt; the diaspora; the arch of Titus commemorates it in Rome.
AD 79Eruption of Vesuvius; destruction of Pompeii and Herculaneum.The famous disaster preserved the cities as documents of Roman life.
AD 96–180The "Five Good Emperors" (Nerva to Marcus Aurelius).The golden age of the Pax Romana.
AD 117Greatest extent of the Roman Empire under Trajan.The empire at its maximum; from Britain to Mesopotamia.
AD 122Hadrian builds Hadrian's Wall.The frontier of Roman Britain; a symbol of imperial consolidation.
AD 180Death of Marcus Aurelius.Conventional end of the Pax Romana; his son Commodus succeeds.

6. Crisis, Recovery & the Dominate (AD 180–337)

DateEventSignificance
AD 193Year of Five Emperors; Septimius Severus founds the Severan dynasty.Military monarchy; commencement of the end of the old order.
AD 212Constitutio Antoniniana: citizenship granted to all free inhabitants.The universalisation of Roman citizenship; end of the old distinction.
AD 235–284The Crisis of the Third Century.Civil war, invasion, plague and inflation; the empire nearly dissolved.
AD 260Capture of the emperor Valerian by the Persians.The humiliation of Rome; the eastern threat became mortal.
AD 260–272The Gallic and Palmyrene breakaway empires.The empire briefly split into three parts.
AD 270–275Reign of Aurelian; reconquest; building of the Aurelian Walls.The beginning of recovery; the "restorer of the world."
AD 284–305Reign of Diocletian; the Tetrarchy; the Great Persecution.Radical reform of the state; division into four; persecution of Christianity.
AD 312Battle of the Milvian Bridge; conversion of Constantine.Constantine's rise; the Christianisation of the empire begun.
AD 313Edict of Milan (religious tolerance for Christianity).End of the persecutions; Christianity's freedom.
AD 325Council of Nicaea.Definition of the Christian creed at the emperor's summons.
AD 330Dedication of Constantinople.Foundation of the new imperial and Christian capital.

7. The Late Empire & the Fall of the West (AD 337–476)

DateEventSignificance
AD 378Battle of Adrianople.Defeat and death of Valens at the hands of the Goths; Rome’s military power shaken.
AD 380Edict of Thessalonica: Christianity made the official religion.The pagan-old Roman religion finally displaced.
AD 395Final division of the empire between Arcadius (east) and Honorius (west).Division of the empire into east and west.
AD 406Barbarians cross the Rhine.The permanent loss of the western provinces begins.
AD 410Visigoths under Alaric sack Rome.Rome falls to an enemy for the first time in 800 years; a profound psychological shock.
AD 451Battle of the Catalaunian Plains (Aetius vs. Attila).Attila the Hun is checked in Gaul.
AD 452Attila invades Italy.Devastation of northern Italy; tradition of the intervention of Pope Leo I.
AD 455Sack of Rome by the Vandals under Gaiseric.The second sack of Rome; the origin of "vandalism."
476 ADDeposition of Romulus Augustulus by Odoacer.The conventional "fall of the Western Roman Empire."
1453Fall of Constantinople to the Ottomans.End of the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire; the end of the Roman state of all.
