The Complete Encyclopedia of Ancient Rome

From the legendary twins Romulus and Remus on the Palatine Hill to the fall of the Western Empire — the full twelve-hundred-year history of Rome, every significant individual, every decisive event, every institution and idea that built the greatest empire of antiquity, each with inline citations and direct links to trusted sources.

How to use this library. This is an encyclopaedia, not a study guide. Every page presents a full chronological narrative of Roman history or a complete reference directory of the people and events that made it. The traditional date for the founding of Rome is 753 BC; the Western Roman Empire fell in AD 476. Each fact is supported by in-text citations pointing to authoritative open sources — primarily the World History Encyclopedia, the Perseus and Loeb digital libraries, Livius, and museum & university resources — with direct, working URLs.

III. Sources & Method

This encyclopaedia is compiled from established scholarly and reference works. The primary online source is the World History Encyclopedia (worldhistory.org), reviewed for educational use by Oxford University and others, alongside the Perseus Digital Library (with the primary sources Livy, Tacitus, Suetonius, Plutarch, Caesar, Cicero and others), Livius, the British Museum, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Direct links to every cited page are given throughout the text.
