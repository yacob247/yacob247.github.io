Every Individual in Roman History

A complete directory of the men and women who built Roman civilisation — kings, consuls, generals, reformers, orators, emperors, empresses, jurists, poets, historians, philosophers and early Christians — with dates, role and significance.

Scope & method. This directory includes the individuals whose lives and works are documented or traditionally recorded as significant in Roman history. Dates are those generally given in standard reference works; "c." indicates approximate dates; reigns of emperors are given where relevant. For full narrative context, follow the links to the corresponding volume.

Numa Pompiliustrad. 715–673 BCSecond king; founder of Rome's religion, calendar and priesthoods. See Vol. 1.
Tullus Hostiliustrad. 673–642 BCWarlike third king; conquered and destroyed Alba Longa.
Ancus Marciustrad. 642–617 BCFourth king; founded the port of Ostia.

Tarquinius Superbus (Tarquin the Proud)trad. 535–509 BCLast king; his tyranny caused the abolition of the monarchy. See Vol. 1.

Lucretiatrad. d. 509 BCNoblewoman whose suicide after dishonour sparked the overthrow of the monarchy.

Mucius Scaevolalegendary (c. 508 BC)Hero who burned his own hand to show Roman resolve before Lars Porsena.
Lars Porsenafl. c. 508 BCEtruscan king of Clusium who attacked Rome on behalf of Tarquin.
Cincinnatustrad. 5th c. BCThe farmer-hero who, called from his plow to be dictator, saved Rome and resigned; symbol of austere civic virtue.
Camillus (Marcus Furius Camillus)c. 446–365 BC"Second founder of Rome"; recovered the city after the Gaulic sack (390 BC); restorer of institutions.
Brennusfl. 390 BCGallic chieftain who sacked Rome and cried "vae victis."

Appius Claudius Caecusfl. 312–279 BCCensor who built the Appian Way and the aqueduct; opponent of the plebeians; "blind" statesman.
Pyrrhus of Epirus319–272 BCGreek king who fought Rome; origin of "Pyrrhic victory." See Vol. 2.
Marcus Atilius Regulusd. c. 250 BCConsul of the First Punic War; captured in Africa; held up as a model of Roman courage and faith.
Hiero IIr. 270–215 BCTyrant (king) of Syracuse; allied with Rome; patron of Archimedes.
Hamilcar Barcac. 275–228 BCCarthaginian general of the First Punic War; conqueror of Spain; father of Hannibal.

Hasdrubal Barcad. 207 BCHannibal's brother; defeated and killed at the Metaurus.

Marcellus (Marcus Claudius Marcellus)c. 268–208 BC"Sword of Rome"; captured Syracuse (212 BC); first to defeat Hannibal in battle.
Scipio Africanus236–183 BCConqueror of Spain; victor of Zama over Hannibal. See Vol. 3.
Scipio Aemilianus Africanus185–129 BCAdopted grandson of Africanus; destroyer of Carthage (146 BC) and Numantia; member of the Scipionic circle.
Cato the Elder (Marcus Porcius Cato)234–149 BCConservator, orator and historian; ended every speech with "Carthage must be destroyed"; champion of old Roman morals.
Archimedesc. 287–212 BCGreek mathematician and engineer who defended Syracuse against Rome (see the Greece Encyclopedia).
Titus Quinctius Flamininusc. 228–174 BCConsul who defeated Philip V and proclaimed the "freedom" of Greece (196 BC).
Lucius Aemilius Paullusc. 229–160 BCVictor of Pydna (168 BC); destroyer of the kingdom of Macedon.
Polybiusc. 200–118 BCGreek historian of the rise of Rome (see also the Greece People volume).
Tiberius Gracchusc. 163–133 BCTribune (133 BC) whose land reform and murder began the era of political violence. See Vol. 4.
Gaius Gracchusc. 154–121 BCTribune (123–122 BC); broader reformer; killed in 121 BC. See Vol. 4.
Gaius Mariusc. 157–86 BCSeven-times consul; creator of the professional army; victor over the Cimbri and Teutones. See Vol. 4.
Lucius Cornelius Sullac. 138–78 BCDictator and proscriptor; marched on Rome; restored the Senate's power. See Vol. 4.
Jugurthac. 160–104 BCNumidian king; defeated by Marius in the Jugurthine War.
Spartacusd. 71 BCThracian gladiator; leader of the great slave revolt (73–71 BC). See Vol. 4.
Marcus Licinius Crassusc. 115–53 BCRichest man in Rome; crushed Spartacus; member of the First Triumvirate; killed at Carrhae.
Pompey the Great (Gnaeus Pompeius)106–48 BCGreat general; destroyer of the pirates and Mithridates VI; rival of Caesar; defeated at Pharsalus. See Vol. 4.
Mithridates VI of Pontusc. 132–63 BCFormidable king; Rome's eastern enemy in three Mithridatic Wars; defeated by Pompey.
Marcus Tullius Cicero106–43 BCThe greatest Roman orator; consul (63 BC, exposed the Catiline conspiracy); philosopher; victim of the proscriptions. See Legacy.
Julius Caesar (Gaius Julius Caesar)100–44 BCConqueror of Gaul; crossed the Rubicon; dictator and reformer; assassinated on the Ides of March. See Vol. 4.
Marcus Junius Brutus85–42 BCAssassin of Caesar; leader of the republicans; defeated at Philippi.
Gaius Cassius Longinusc. 85–42 BCCo-leader of the conspiracy against Caesar; defeated at Philippi.
Mark Antony (Marcus Antonius)83–30 BCCaesar's lieutenant; member of the Second Triumvirate; partner of Cleopatra; defeated at Actium.
Cleopatra VII69–30 BCLast queen of Ptolemaic Egypt; allied with Caesar and Antony (see the Greece Encyclopedia).
Marcus Aemilius Lepidusc. 89–13 BCMember of the Second Triumvirate; pontifex maximus; stripped of power by Octavian.
Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa63–12 BCAugustus' greatest general and friend; victor at Actium; builder of the first Pantheon.

C. The Emperors (27 BC–AD 476)

NameReignRole & Significance
Augustus (Octavian)27 BC–AD 14First emperor; founder of the Principate and of the Roman Empire. See Vol. 5.
TiberiusAD 14–37Second emperor; capable but sombre and increasingly tyrannical.
CaligulaAD 37–41Byword for mad tyranny; assassinated by the Praetorians.
ClaudiusAD 41–54Unlikely but capable emperor; conquered Britain (43).
NeroAD 54–68Last Julio-Claudian; notorious for the Great Fire of Rome and the persecution of Christians; suicide in 68.
Galba, Otho, VitelliusAD 69The three short-lived emperors of the Year of the Four Emperors.
VespasianAD 69–79Founder of the Flavian dynasty; began the Colosseum.
TitusAD 79–81Completed the Colosseum; his reign saw the eruption of Vesuvius.
DomitianAD 81–96Efficient but tyrannical; last Flavian; assassinated.
NervaAD 96–98Elderly senator; first of the Five Good Emperors.
TrajanAD 98–117The best of the emperors in justice and conquest; brought the empire to its greatest extent.
HadrianAD 117–138Consolidator; built Hadrian's Wall and rebuilt the Pantheon.
Antoninus PiusAD 138–161Peaceable administrator of the high empire.
Marcus AureliusAD 161–180Philosopher-emperor; Stoic author of the Meditations; defender of the empire.
CommodusAD 180–192Son of Marcus Aurelius; decadent and despotic; marked the end of the Pax Romana.
Septimius SeverusAD 193–211African founder of the Severan dynasty; military autocrat.
CaracallaAD 198–217Granted citizenship to all free inhabitants (212); tyrant; built the baths that bear his name.
Elagabalus / Alexander SeverusAD 218–235Latter Severans; their murder in 235 began the Crisis of the Third Century.
Claudius II GothicusAD 268–270Soldier-emperor who checked the Goths.
AurelianAD 270–275"Restorer of the world"; reconquered the breakaway empires; built the Aurelian Walls.
DiocletianAD 284–305Founder of the Tetrarchy and the Dominate; great reformer; abdicated. See Vol. 6.
Constantine I the GreatAD 306–337First Christian emperor; founder of Constantinople; summoned Nicaea. See Vol. 6.
Julian the ApostateAD 361–363Last pagan emperor; sought to restore the old religion; died in Persia.
ValensAD 364–378Eastern emperor killed at Adrianople (378) by the Goths.
Gratian / Valentinian IIAD 367–392Western emperors of the late fourth century.
Theodosius I the GreatAD 379–395Last emperor of a united empire; made Christianity official; divided the empire on his death.
Arcadius / HonoriusAD 395–423Emperors of east and west after the division; weak rulers during the great invasions.
HonoriusAD 395–423Western emperor during the sack of Rome (410), ruling from Ravenna.
Valentinian IIIAD 425–455Western emperor; murdered in 455, the eve of the Vandal sack.
Romulus AugustulusAD 475–476Last western emperor; deposed by Odoacer in 476; the traditional "fall of Rome." See Vol. 7.

NameDateRole & Significance

Agrippina the YoungerAD 15–59Mother of Nero; powerful and ruthless; murdered on Nero's orders.
